# Q8: Staging and Acting
**Opens Class 10 · Covers Class 9 (Deck 09) and H6 · 5 drawn from 15**

---

### Q8.01 · MC
A critique note says a pose is "stiff." That is usually:
- * An acting note, not a drawing note
- A note about line quality
- A note about anatomy
- A note about the panel's composition
> Source: Deck 09, the note you'll get.

### Q8.02 · MC
The core question to ask of any panel with a character in it:
- * What does this character want, right now, in this shot
- Is the anatomy correct
- Is the character on model
- Does the pose match the previous panel
> Source: Deck 09, the core question. H6.

### Q8.03 · MC
If you cannot answer that question for a panel, the panel is:
- * Decoration
- Incomplete
- A reaction shot
- An establishing shot
> Source: Deck 09.

### Q8.04 · MC
An animator acts across time. A board artist acts in:
- * Single held moments, picking the one frame that carries the beat
- Sequences of at least twelve drawings
- Dialogue, primarily
- Camera movement
> Source: Deck 09. CURRICULUM.md boundary with Acting for Animation.

### Q8.05 · MC
The line of action is:
- * One curve through the whole figure, drawn before anything else
- The path a character walks through the scene
- The 180 degree line
- The direction the character is looking
> Source: Deck 09. H6.

### Q8.06 · MC
A figure built only from curves reads as:
- * Mush, with no tension holding it
- Dynamic and energetic
- Rigid and formal
- Correctly constructed
> Source: Deck 09, straight against curve.

### Q8.07 · MC
The arm crosses in front of the torso and disappears from the silhouette. The cheapest fix:
- * Move the arm off the body to open a gap
- Redraw the arm with more detail
- Darken the torso
- Change the shot size
> Source: Deck 09, negative space in the pose.

### Q8.08 · MC
Characters who read as "stickers" pasted onto the background usually lack:
- * Weight, meaning the contact with the ground and the mass above it
- Detail in the costume
- Correct proportions
- A background that matches
> Source: Deck 09, weight.

### Q8.09 · MC
An off-balance pose is useful in a board because:
- * A body past its own centre of gravity has already committed to something
- It is more difficult to draw
- It fills the frame better
- It reads as comedic
> Source: Deck 09, weight diagram.

### Q8.10 · MC
Symmetrical poses read as:
- * Dead, formal or robotic, which is sometimes what you want
- Natural and relaxed
- Dynamic
- Off balance
> Source: Deck 09, asymmetry.

### Q8.11 · MC
Deck 09 says to draw the face last. Why?
- * The body carries most of the performance, and a face drawn first forces the pose to accommodate it
- Faces are the hardest part
- Faces are rarely visible at board scale
- It saves time
> Source: Deck 09, faces last. H6 checklist.

### Q8.12 · MC
At board scale, a complete readable expression needs about:
- * Three marks: brow, eye line, mouth
- A fully rendered face
- Eyes and mouth only
- Six to eight marks
> Source: Deck 09, expression economy.

### Q8.13 · MC
If you learn one facial feature properly, Deck 09 says learn:
- * The brow
- The mouth
- The eyes
- The jaw
> Source: Deck 09. Same eyes and mouth, four brows, four emotions.

### Q8.14 · MC
Beginners board the person talking. Where is the performance usually?
- * On the face of the person listening
- On the speaker, correctly
- In the camera movement
- In the background staging
> Source: Deck 09, stage the listener. Acting is reacting.

### Q8.15 · MC
Two characters stand in fixed positions for twelve panels while only their faces change. That is:
- * Radio with pictures, and worth asking why
- Correct for a dialogue scene
- Good continuity practice
- Required by the 180 degree rule
> Source: Deck 09, blocking across a sequence.
