### Q4.01 · MC
The 180 degree rule says:
- * Keep the camera on one side of an imaginary line drawn through the action
- Never rotate the camera more than 180 degrees in a single move
- Always cut on a 180 degree change of angle
- Reverse the camera exactly across the line on every cut
> Source: Deck 08a. H4.

### Q4.02 · MC
Two characters are talking. A is frame left, B is frame right. You cut, and now A is frame right. What happened?
- * The line was crossed
- The eyeline was matched
- The action was matched
- Screen direction was preserved
> Source: Deck 08a. H4.

### Q4.03 · MC
Your panel crosses the line. What is the fix?
- * Move the camera back to the correct side of the line
- Redraw the characters in swapped positions
- Add a wider establishing shot afterward
- Flip the panel horizontally
> Source: Deck 08a exit exercise. Class 4's whole lesson.

### Q4.05 · MC
An eyeline match means:
- * The direction a character looks in one shot agrees with where the thing they are looking at sits in the next
- Two characters are the same height in frame
- Both characters look directly at the camera
- The horizon lines match between shots
> Source: Deck 08a. H4.

### Q4.06 · MC
A character walks left to right. In the next shot they walk right to left, with no cue in between. The audience reads this as:
- * They turned around and are heading back
- Time has passed
- A new location
- Nothing, it is invisible
> Source: H4, screen direction. Reversed direction reads as reversed journey.

### Q4.07 · MC
Match on action means:
- * Cutting mid-movement so the action carries the eye across the cut
- Cutting at the end of a movement, once it has finished
- Matching the lighting between two shots
- Repeating the same framing on both sides of the cut
> Source: Deck 08a. H4.

### Q4.08 · MC
A match cut between two identical framings produces:
- * A jump cut
- A smooth transition
- A cut-in
- A cross-cut
> Source: H4. EX5 rubric, strip 2.

### Q4.09 · MC
What is a floor plan, in this course?
- * An overhead diagram of the space with the camera positions numbered and the line marked
- A scale drawing of the set for construction
- The lighting layout
- The order the shots will be filmed in
> Source: Deck 08a, live demo. Introduced Class 4.

### Q4.10 · MC
The floor plan turns up again in the perspective study, the lighting study, the final's shot list, and the games material. That repetition is:
- * Deliberate, because it is one tool the course keeps pointing at by name
- Coincidence
- A duplication that should be cleaned up
- Specific to film rather than games
> Source: Deck 08a, forward connection. CLAUDE.md, systems thinking.

### Q4.11 · MC
Drawing a floor plan before boarding a sequence mostly prevents:
- * Crossed lines, before a single panel is drawn
- Composition errors
- Poor lighting choices
- Timing mistakes
> Source: Deck 08a. PR4 rubric, the floor plan criterion.

### Q4.12 · MC
Shot / reverse shot describes:
- * Alternating between two complementary angles on a conversation
- Reversing the running order of a sequence
- Repeating a shot later for emphasis
- Cutting from an action to its consequence
> Source: Deck 08a. H2.

### Q4.14 · MC
PR1 City Park is deliberately constrained to one location and two characters. Why?
- * Continuity is harder to hold across complexity, and continuity is the entire subject
- Simple sequences are faster to grade
- Students cannot handle more characters
- It matches the length of a standard animatic
> Source: PR1 brief, "why it's this boring on purpose."

### Q4.15 · MC
Adding a car chase to the City Park sequence:
- * Usually costs marks, because it makes the thing being assessed harder to demonstrate
- Earns marks for ambition
- Is required for full credit on staging
- Has no effect either way
> Source: PR1 brief and rubric, "what is not graded."
