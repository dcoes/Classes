### Q3.03 · MC
What actually makes a shot a low angle?
- * The camera is below the eyeline, so the horizon line in the frame has moved down
- The character is looking upward
- The character's head is tilted back
- The frame is taller than it is wide
> Source: Deck 03 camera angles. EX3 rubric names this the costliest failure.

### Q3.04 · TF
If your low angle and your eye-level shot share the same horizon line, the camera never actually moved.
- * True
- False
> Source: EX3 rubric. Deck 06 opens on this.

### Q3.05 · MC
A Dutch angle is achieved by:
- * Canting the camera so the horizon tilts
- Tilting the subject within an upright frame
- Shooting from below the eyeline
- Rotating the subject away from camera
> Source: Deck 03. EX3 rubric, "a tilted subject is not a Dutch angle."

### Q3.06 · MC
In an over the shoulder shot:
- * A shoulder or head occupies the near foreground
- Both characters are seen in profile
- The camera looks straight down on the subject
- The viewing character is absent from frame
> Source: Deck 03. H2.

### Q3.07 · MC
The camera physically moves toward the subject on a track. The dolly shot does something a zoom does not. What?
- * The spatial relationship between foreground and background changes
- The subject gets larger in frame
- The shot gets brighter
- The focus stays locked
> Source: Deck 03 camera movement.

### Q3.09 · MC
A pan is:
- * The camera rotating horizontally from a fixed position

> Source: Deck 03 camera movement. H2.

### Q3.10 · MC
On EX3, all twelve shots must be of the same subject in the same location. Why?
- * With one variable, you can tell what the camera change actually did
- It is faster to draw
- It matches the way coverage is scheduled on set
- It makes the panels easier to grade
> Source: EX3 brief, "the constraint is the assignment."

### Q3.11 · MC
EX3 asks for a one-line caption under each panel. What should it say?
- * What the shot is for
- Where the camera is
- Which lens would be used
- How long the shot should hold
> Source: EX3 brief and rubric, the captions criterion.

### Q3.14 · MC
EX3 asks for a thirteenth panel showing the wrong shot for the moment. What is it for?
- * It is the first time in the course you defend a choice, and arguing against a bad shot is easier than arguing for a good one
- It tests whether you can draw badly on purpose
- It fills the last slot on the template
- It demonstrates a shot type not covered in H2
> Source: EX3 brief, panel 13.

### Q3.15 · MC
When picking a location for EX3, you need to plan for OTS and POV specifically because:

> Source: EX3 brief, the warning before you choose.
