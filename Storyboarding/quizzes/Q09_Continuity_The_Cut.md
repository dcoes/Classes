# Q9: Continuity, The Cut
**Opens Class 11 · Covers Class 10 (Deck 08b), H4, and EX5 · 5 drawn from 15**

---

### Q9.01 · MC
A cut-in moves:
- * Closer into the same space, to a detail that was already there
- Away from the main action to somewhere else
- To a character reacting inside the scene
- Between two locations in alternation
> Source: Deck 08b. EX5 rubric, strip 4.

### Q9.02 · MC
EX5 requires you to circle the cut-in detail on the wide panel. Why?
- * That circle is the definition: if you cannot find it in the wide, it is not a cut-in
- It shows the panel order
- It marks the focal point for the printer
- It indicates where the camera moves next
> Source: EX5 brief, strip 4.

### Q9.03 · MC
A cut-away, by definition:
- * Leaves the space of the main action
- Goes closer into the main action
- Shows a character responding to the main action
- Repeats a previous shot
> Source: Deck 08b. EX5 rubric.

### Q9.04 · MC
A bus speeds past. You cut to people at the bus stop watching it, then back. That is:
- * A reaction shot, because it never leaves the scene's space or time
- A cut-away
- A cut-in
- A cross-cut
> Source: EX5 brief and rubric, the cut-away rebuilt. This is the distinction the assignment exists to teach.

### Q9.05 · MC
EX5 asks you to name the cut-away's job. The two options are:
- * Buys time, or gives the audience what the character doesn't have
- Adds variety, or breaks up a long take
- Establishes location, or shows scale
- Covers an editing mistake, or extends a scene
> Source: EX5 brief, strip 5.

### Q9.06 · MC
A character waits, we cut to an empty depot elsewhere, then back to the character. The audience now knows the bus is not coming and the character does not. That gap is:
- * Dramatic irony
- A continuity error
- A jump cut
- A match cut
> Source: EX5 brief, strip 5, the second job.

### Q9.07 · MC
For a match on action to work, the cut must land:
- * Mid-movement, not at the end of it
- Exactly as the movement completes
- Before the movement begins
- On a held pose
> Source: H4. EX5 rubric, strip 2.

### Q9.08 · MC
The second requirement for a match cut in EX5 is that:
- * The frame size changes noticeably across the cut
- The camera angle stays identical
- The lighting matches
- The action reverses direction
> Source: EX5 brief, strip 2. Two identical framings give a jump cut.

### Q9.09 · MC
Cross-cutting means:
- * Alternating between two locations, usually converging
- Cutting across the 180 degree line
- Cutting between two shot sizes of the same subject
- Cutting on a diagonal composition
> Source: Deck 08b. EX5, strip 6.

### Q9.10 · TF
Cross-cutting is the one device on the EX5 list that cannot be demonstrated in a single panel or a pair.
- * True
- False
> Source: EX5 brief, strip 6. It needs the rhythm, and the rhythm needs four panels.

### Q9.11 · MC
EX5 strip 3 asks for a fourth panel labelled BROKEN, crossing the line on purpose. It is graded as:
- * Correct work, because producing the error deliberately proves you can see it
- A deduction, since it violates the rule
- Optional extra credit
- A formatting requirement only
> Source: EX5 brief and rubric.

### Q9.12 · MC
The EX5 naming test is:
- * Cover the labels, and check whether a stranger can sort the six strips to the six device names
- Check that each strip has the right number of panels
- Confirm the devices appear in the order taught
- Verify the notation matches H1
> Source: EX5 brief, how it is scored.

### Q9.13 · MC
EX5 is placed after City Park rather than before because:
- * The rules arrive once as instruction and once as retrieval, several classes apart, which is how they stick
- Students need the drawing practice first
- The template is not ready earlier
- City Park is graded on different rules
> Source: EX5 brief and rubric, the closing note.

### Q9.14 · MC
On EX5 strip 1, the short and tall characters reverse across the cut. What must NOT happen?
- * Their left and right screen positions swapping
- The camera angle changing
- The frame size changing
- The eyelines meeting
> Source: EX5 rubric, per-strip checks.

### Q9.15 · MC
Six unrelated fragments, not connected into a story, is:
- * The correct answer for EX5
- A misreading of the brief
- Acceptable but scores lower
- Only allowed if noted in the submission
> Source: EX5 brief, "what is not graded."
