# Q7: Lighting
**Opens Class 9 · Covers Class 7 (Deck 07), H7, and EX6 · 5 drawn from 15**

---

### Q7.01 · MC
H7 gives five questions that describe the lighting of any frame. Which is NOT one of them?
- * What time of day is it
- Where is the key
- Hard or soft
- Motivated or stylized
> Source: H7, reading any lit frame. The five are key direction, high/low key, contrast, hard/soft, motivated/stylized.

### Q7.02 · MC
In a three-point setup, the fill light's job is to:
- * Lift the shadow the key creates so it is not solid black
- Separate the subject from the background
- Provide the main modelling on the face
- Light the background
> Source: Deck 07, three-point. H7.

### Q7.03 · MC
The back light exists to:
- * Separate the subject from the ground
- Add warmth to the skin
- Fill the shadow side
- Create the main shadow shapes
> Source: Deck 07. H7. This is figure and ground separation done with a lamp.

### Q7.04 · MC
"High key" describes:
- * How much shadow is present, not how much light
- How bright the key light is
- The colour temperature of the key
- The height of the key above the subject
> Source: H7, the two dials. EX6 rubric names this the costliest failure.

### Q7.05 · MC
A dim, flat, foggy frame is:
- * Low contrast, and nowhere near high key
- High key, because it has no dense shadows
- Low key, because it is dark
- Neither, since contrast only applies to bright images
> Source: H7, the 2x2 table.

### Q7.06 · TF
Level and contrast are independent dials, which is why high key and low contrast are separate items on the EX6 list.
- * True
- False
> Source: H7. EX6 rubric.

### Q7.07 · MC
What makes light hard rather than soft?
- * The apparent size of the source relative to the subject
- The wattage of the source
- The distance of the source from the camera
- The colour of the source
> Source: H7, hard and soft.

### Q7.08 · MC
Moving a lamp closer to the subject makes the light:
- * Softer, because the source is now larger relative to the subject
- Harder, because it is more intense
- Unchanged in quality, only in brightness
- Cooler in colour
> Source: H7. Apparent size is the controlling variable.

### Q7.09 · MC
"Motivated" lighting means:
- * The frame contains or implies a source that explains the light
- The lighting supports the character's emotional state
- The lighting was planned before the shoot
- The key comes from a practical lamp only
> Source: H7, motivated or stylized.

### Q7.10 · MC
Underlighting reads as wrong to audiences with no film education at all because:
- * Faces are almost never lit from below in nature
- It is technically difficult to achieve
- It was banned under the production code
- It desaturates skin tones
> Source: Deck 07, direction of light. H7.

### Q7.11 · MC
Side lighting is the most legible setup at board scale because:
- * The value split across the subject is itself a shape
- It uses the fewest lights
- It flattens the form
- It removes the need for fill
> Source: H7, the six setups.

### Q7.12 · MC
EX6 requires a three-value reduction of every setup. What is that for?
- * It makes a photo, a render and a drawing the same class of object, so the criteria do not care what made the image
- It is easier to print
- It simulates black and white film
- It reduces file size
> Source: EX6 brief and rubric, the three constraints.

### Q7.13 · MC
The EX6 naming test asks whether:
- * A stranger can sort the six reductions to the six setup names with the labels covered
- The student can name all six setups from memory
- The lighting matches a reference film
- The images were made in the same medium
> Source: EX6 brief. Same logic as the silhouette test.

### Q7.14 · MC
EX6 requires one subject across all six setups because:
- * With the subject fixed, the light is the only variable and the six become comparable
- Six subjects would take too long
- The rubric requires a single medium
- It matches the supplied PSD
> Source: EX6 brief, constraint 1.

### Q7.15 · MC
On EX6, a phone photograph of a mug lit six ways with a desk lamp, diagrammed correctly:
- * Is a full-marks submission
- Scores lower than a drawn version
- Is accepted only as an accommodation
- Requires prior approval
> Source: EX6 brief and rubric. Medium carries no scoring weight.
