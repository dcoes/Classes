# H5 — Timing & Hold-Length Reference
**CA 140 Storyboarding · Issued Session 13, with the Animatic assignment**

> Everything before the animatic was about what's in the frame. This is about how long it stays there — and it's where most student projects fall apart.

---

## Hold-length reference

| Shot type | Typical hold |
|---|---|
| Establishing shot | 3–4 sec |
| Wide, new information | 2–3 sec |
| Dialogue beat | ~2 sec |
| Reaction | 0.75–1.5 sec |
| Insert / detail | 0.5–1 sec |
| Action cut | as fast as it reads |
| Beat of silence (dramatic) | 2–5 sec |

**These are starting points, not rules — but you need something to deviate from. Guessing from zero is why timing feels random.**

---

## Frame rate is the ruler

- **24 fps** — film
- **30 fps** — broadcast
- **Games** — vary
- **High-speed capture** — enables slow motion

**Pick one at the start and never change it.**

---

## The two failures

**Too long:** pace drags, audience bored.
**Too short:** ambiguous, audience confused.

Both look like "bad animatic" and they have opposite fixes.

---

## Reading time

Any panel with text, or with information the audience must *find*, needs longer than you think. **Time it by reading it aloud twice.**

---

## The stopwatch

Every animator should own a stopwatch and most never use one, because they think they already know how long things take.

They don't. Neither do you.

---

## Video reference

Shoot it on your phone. Act it out. Time it. Reference makes any artist better at any skill level — this is not a beginner's crutch.

---

## Iterate

An NLE makes adding or removing frames trivial. **Your first timing pass will be wrong. Budget for three passes.**

## Prep, before any of the above

**Working resolution.** 1920 × 1080 baseline for 16:9. Work at 2× if you plan pushes or pans — you can scale down, not up.

**Document setup.** Panels at the delivery ratio from your template assignment. Separate documents or artboards, **not one big page.** The page layout is for the printed board; the animatic wants individual frames.

**Naming and numbering.**
```
SEQ01_SH010.png
SEQ01_SH020.png
```
**Increment by ten** so you can insert shots later without renumbering everything.

**Export.** PNG for lossless with transparency. JPG only for flattened stills you'll never re-edit. Never GIF. Keep the PSD *and* the PNG — PSD keeps your layers, PNG is what you hand to After Effects.

**Layer separation for motion.** If an arm waves, a character crosses, or the camera pushes past a foreground element, that element needs its own layer — **with the hole behind it filled.**

**Pans and pushes.** For a pan, draw wider than the frame. For a push, draw at higher resolution than the frame. **You cannot invent detail you didn't draw. Plan the move before you draw the panel.**

---

## Sound

**Temp audio changes everything.** Play twenty seconds of your animatic silent, then with temp music. It is not the same piece of work. This is the single cheapest upgrade available to you.

**What to use:** temp music · ambience · a few key effects · scratch dialogue you record yourself on your phone. Scratch dialogue recorded badly by you beats no dialogue — timing needs a voice to time against.

**Sourcing legally:** Freesound, YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay, Incompetech, CC-licensed tracks. **Credit your sources in your submission.** Industry temp tracks get pulled from released films and replaced later; for your portfolio, use licensed or CC material so you can actually show the work.
