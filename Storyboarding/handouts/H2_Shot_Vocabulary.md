# H2 — Shot Vocabulary Reference
**CA 140 Storyboarding · Issued Session 3 · Keep this for the midterm**

> The midterm asks you to name shots from an observed sequence. This is the list you're being tested against.

---

## Shot size

Measured by how much of the subject fills the frame.

| Abbrev. | Shot | What's in frame | Typically used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XLS / ELS** | Extreme Long Shot | A landscape, a skyline, a location. Figures tiny or absent. | Scale, isolation, establishing a world |
| **LS / WS** | Long Shot / Wide Shot | Full figure with substantial environment | Where are we, who's there, what's happening |
| **FS** | Full Shot | Head near top of frame, feet near bottom | Full-body action and staging |
| **MLS** | Medium Long Shot | Knees up | The compromise between action and expression |
| **MS** | Medium Shot | Waist up | Dialogue. The most common shot in film. |
| **MCU** | Medium Close-Up | Chest up | Dialogue with more emotional weight |
| **CU** | Close-Up | Shoulders to top of head | Subtlety of expression |
| **ECU / XCU** | Extreme Close-Up | A detail: an eye, a mouth, a doorknob | Tension, mystery, emphasis |

**By population, not size:**

| Abbrev. | Shot | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **1-SHOT** | Single | One character in frame |
| **2-SHOT** | Two shot | Two characters sharing the frame |
| **3-SHOT** | Three shot | Three |
| **GRP** | Group shot | More than three |
| **INS** | Insert | A close detail cut into a wider master |

**At long shot sizes, exaggerate the movement.** A gesture that reads in a medium shot disappears in a wide one.

## Camera angle

Where the camera is relative to the subject.

| Angle | Camera position | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **Eye Level** | Level with the subject's eyes | Neutral; the audience meets them as an equal |
| **High Angle** | Above, tilted down | Diminished, vulnerable, observed |
| **Low Angle** | Below, tilted up | Dominant, large, threatening |
| **Bird's Eye** | Directly overhead | The audience sees what the character can't |
| **Worm's Eye** | Directly below | Extreme scale, disorientation |
| **Canted / Dutch** | Tilted off-axis | Unstable, unhinged, wrong |
| **Three-Quarter (45°)** | Between frontal and profile | Strongest general-purpose angle — gives depth |
| **Frontal** | Straight on | Flat, but intimate in close-up; used for subjective shots |
| **Profile** | Directly from the side | Observational, formal |
| **OTS** | Over the shoulder | Puts you in one character's position in a conversation |

**Angle and staging stack.** A low angle on a character who is *also* physically higher in the frame is redundant. Sometimes you want the contradiction instead.

## Camera movement

| Notation | Move | The camera... |
|---|---|---|
| **PAN** | Pan | pivots horizontally. Does not travel. |
| **TILT** | Tilt | pivots vertically. Does not travel. |
| **TRUCK / DOLLY** | Dolly | travels toward or away from the subject. |
| **TRACK** | Tracking | travels alongside the subject. |
| **CRANE / BOOM** | Crane | travels vertically through space. |
| **ZOOM** | Zoom | stays still; the lens magnifies. |
| **STEADICAM** | Steadicam | follows freely and smoothly, handheld-mounted. |
| **ZOLLY** | Dolly zoom | trucks one way while zooming the other. Background changes size, subject doesn't. |
| **SWISH PAN** | Swish pan | pans fast enough to blur — a dramatic shift between two things. |

**Dolly ≠ zoom.** A dolly changes your relationship to the space. A zoom is a crop. This distinction is on the midterm.

---

## Camera perspective

| Perspective | Whose eyes |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Nobody's. The camera observes from the sidelines. |
| **Point of View (POV)** | Near a specific character's viewpoint — you see roughly what they see. |
| **Subjective** | The camera *is* the character. First-person games live here permanently. |

---

## Naming a shot is not choosing a shot

You will be graded on both, but they're different skills. The vocabulary lets you describe a decision quickly. It does not make the decision. When you're stuck on which shot to use, the question is never "which one is correct" — it's **what does the audience need to know right now, and what do I want them to feel about it.**
