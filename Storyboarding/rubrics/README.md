# Rubrics — CA 140 Storyboarding

Twelve rubrics, 770 points, plus 16 points of exit tickets = **786 total.**

Every rubric here has a matching brief in [`../assignments/`](../assignments/) under the same filename. Session placement is in [`../SESSION_MAP.md`](../SESSION_MAP.md).

---

## The two bands

**EX — skill exercises.** Single-stage. No milestone cap. Short rubrics with objectively checkable criteria: a measurement, a count, a naming test. Cheap to grade and low-stakes to fail, which is what lets a student who has never drawn find their footing before anything expensive is due.

**PR — projects.** Milestone-graded, with the 50% cascading cap. These carry the weight.

Band EX is 28% of the grade, band PR is 70%, exit tickets are 2%. Milestone-graded work stays the dominant force, which is what keeps the cap meaningful.

## What is graded, everywhere

**Clarity and judgment, not rendering.** The question is never "is this well drawn." It is "does this communicate, and did you decide it on purpose." Drawing is treated as *notation* throughout.

**Silhouette readability** is the recurring standard — introduced Session 1 and named explicitly in EX2's downstream requirements, PR1 and PR4. Deliberately independent of skill level, which is what makes it fair.

Three rubrics use the same fairness mechanism under different names, and it's worth seeing them as one thing:

| Assignment | The test |
|---|---|
| Every panel, all term | **Silhouette test** — fill it black; can a stranger name the action? |
| EX5 Continuity Strips | **Naming test** — cover the labels; can a stranger sort the six devices? |
| EX6 Lighting Study | **Naming test** — cover the labels; can a stranger sort the six setups? |

Each asks whether the work *reads*, not whether it's well made, and each can be run by the student before submitting.

## Medium neutrality

**EX6 accepts any medium** — drawing, photography, 3D render, value study — and scores them identically. The mechanism is the three-value reduction: a posterized photo, render and drawing are the same class of object, so the criteria don't care what made the image.

**EX4 offers a photo-first track** at equal credit. **PR2** grades observation boards where stick figures in the right positions beat beautiful figures in the wrong ones.

This is not accommodation. These assignments assess identifying and controlling something — light, depth, shot grammar — and drawing ability is not that skill. Grading it as though it were is the failure mode these rubrics are built to avoid.

---

## The milestone cap

Every PR assignment uses the same rule, stated in its own rubric:

> **Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at 50% of that stage's available points.**

The cap cascades, so skipping one stage costs its points *and* half the next stage's. On PR4 that is 77.5 points off 200 for skipping two stages. The arithmetic is designed to make skipping process irrational rather than merely discouraged (`PROJECT.md` §5), and it is the structural answer to work a student didn't do themselves — process is what's assessed.

**Band EX carries no cap.** Those are single-stage by design.

## A note on CO5

*Explain the creative work of self and peers* was the least-assessed objective in this course, because critique participation was informal and ungraded.

Four things now carry it: **PR5 The Pitch** (which exists largely for this reason), **peer notes** during pitch and critique sessions, the **written analysis** on PR2, and **EX6's closing paragraph**.

The last one matters more than its two points suggest — it means CO5 is now assessed in a low-stakes exercise *before* the midterm, rather than appearing for the first time on a hundred-point assignment.
