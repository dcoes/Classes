# Rubrics: CA 140 Storyboarding

Twelve rubrics. **Every one totals 100% of its own assignment.** Course weighting is a separate layer and lives in [`../syllabus-language.md`](../syllabus-language.md).

Each rubric has a matching brief in [`../assignments/`](../assignments/) under the same filename. Class placement is in [`../SESSION_MAP.md`](../SESSION_MAP.md).

---

## Built for Canvas

Every criterion carries **pre-populated bands you click**, each with a one-line justification:

```
Silhouette readability, 20% of this assignment
  Exemplary   20%  Every pose reads filled solid black.
  Good        17%  Reads throughout, one or two ambiguous poses.
  Average     14%  Reads about half the time; limbs lost in the torso.
  Developing  10%  Most poses don't read; action inferred from the caption.
  Missing      0%  Not submitted.
```

The bands are the fast path with forty submissions open. The longer prose under each criterion is for the student, and explains what the band language means.

Band values are roughly full / 85% / 70% / 50% / 0 of the criterion's weight, rounded to whole numbers so Canvas totals stay clean.

## What is graded, everywhere

**Clarity and judgment, not rendering.** The question is never "is this well drawn." It is "does this communicate, and did you decide it on purpose." Drawing is treated as *notation* throughout.

Three rubrics use the same fairness mechanism under different names, and it's worth seeing them as one thing:

| Assignment | The test |
|---|---|
| Every panel, all term | **Silhouette test.** Fill it black. Can a stranger name the action? |
| EX5 Continuity Strips | **Naming test.** Cover the labels. Can a stranger sort the six devices? |
| EX6 Lighting Study | **Naming test.** Cover the labels. Can a stranger sort the six setups? |

Each asks whether the work *reads*, not whether it's well made, and each can be run by the student before submitting.

---

## Medium neutrality

**EX6 accepts any medium**, drawing, photography, 3D render or value study, and scores them identically. The mechanism is the three-value reduction: a posterized photo, render and drawing are the same class of object, so the criteria don't care what made the image. **EX4** offers a photo-first track at equal credit. **PR2** grades observation boards where stick figures in the right positions beat beautiful figures in the wrong ones.

This is not accommodation. These assignments assess identifying and controlling something (light, depth, shot grammar) and drawing ability is not that skill. Grading it as though it were is the failure mode these rubrics are built to avoid.

## The milestone cap

Every PR rubric states it:

> **Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at half its available percentage.**

The cap cascades, so skipping one stage costs its weight *and* half the next stage's. On PR4 that is a 38-point hit on a 100-point rubric for skipping two stages. The arithmetic is designed to make skipping process irrational rather than merely discouraged, and it is the structural answer to work a student didn't do themselves: process is what's assessed.

**EX rubrics carry no cap.** Those are single-stage by design.

## A note on CO5

*Explain the creative work of self and peers* was the least-assessed objective in this course, because critique participation was informal and ungraded.

Four things now carry it: **PR5 The Pitch**, which exists largely for this reason, **peer notes** during pitch and critique classes, the **written analysis** on PR2, and **EX6's closing paragraph**. The last matters more than its 5% suggests, because it puts CO5 into a low-stakes exercise before the midterm rather than having it appear for the first time on the largest assignment in the course.

## A note on EX7

Its objective claim is deliberately thin, CO1 only and weakly. It is a tool-fluency exercise, which is why it's 3% and near-free if attempted. Better to say that plainly than to manufacture a mapping that doesn't survive scrutiny.
