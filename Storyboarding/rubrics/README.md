## What is graded, everywhere

**Clarity and judgment, not rendering.** The question is never "is this well drawn." It is "does this communicate, and did you decide it on purpose." Drawing is treated as *notation* throughout.

**Silhouette readability** is the recurring standard — introduced Session 1 and named explicitly in A1, City Park, and the Final. It is deliberately independent of skill level, which is what makes it fair.

## The milestone cap

Every multi-stage assignment uses the same rule, stated in its own rubric:

> **Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at 50% of that stage's available points.**

The cap cascades, so skipping one stage costs you its points *and* half of the next stage's. The arithmetic is designed to make skipping process irrational rather than merely discouraged (PROJECT.md §5), and it is the structural answer to AI-generated submissions — process is what's assessed.

## A note on CO5

*Explain the creative work of self and peers* was the least-assessed objective in this course, because critique participation was informal and ungraded. Three things now carry it: **The Pitch** (which exists largely for this reason), **peer notes** during pitch sessions, and the **written analysis** on the midterm. That is the strongest argument for the pitch assignment, and it should be said out loud when the assignment is introduced.
