# EX5 Continuity Strips: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 4% of the course grade · Band EX**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX5_Continuity_Strips.md`](../assignments/EX5_Continuity_Strips.md)
**Assessed against CO1** (*planning tool*), **CO2** (*film terminology*) **and CO3** (*tabulating storyboards from events*).

> Six devices, six strips, twenty panels, on the 1.85 : 1 template. Demonstrating grammar on demand, which is the difference between having read about a device and being able to execute it.

## 2 . Each device is the device: 30%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 30 | Labels covered, a stranger sorts all six strips to the six names. Cut-in and cut-away are clearly different things. |
| Good | 26 | Five of six read. |
| Average | 21 | Four of six read. |
| Developing | 15 | Two or three read. |
| Missing | 0 | Devices indistinguishable from each other. |

Five points a strip. The per-strip checks:

| # | Device | It counts when |
|--:|---|---|
| 1 | Height reverse | Both cameras on one side of the line, and screen positions **do not swap** between panels 2 and 3 |
| 2 | Match on action | Frame size changes noticeably **and** the cut lands mid-movement |
| 3 | OTS shot/reverse | Clean pair, **and** panel 4 is labelled BROKEN and actually crosses the line |
| 4 | Cut-in | The detail is **circled in the wide panel** |
| 5 | Cut-away | Panel 2 is somewhere the main action isn't, and panel 3 shows time has passed |
| 6 | Cross-cutting | Four panels, A/B/A/B, converging on something |

## The distinction that carries the most weight

**Cut-in, cut-away and reaction shot are three different tools**, and students routinely submit the same thing three times.

- A **cut-in** goes closer into the same space. The detail was always in the wide, which is why the circle on panel 1 is required and why a strip without it can't take full marks however good the panels are.
- A **cut-away** leaves the space. Its job is to buy time or to hand the audience something the character doesn't have.
- A **reaction shot** stays inside the scene and shows someone responding. Useful, common, and neither of the above.

A student who submits a bystander watching the bus has drawn a reaction shot and labelled it a cutaway. That costs the strip 5 points and usually the naming line too, because a reaction shot can't do either job the naming asks for.

## Why the BROKEN panel is graded, not penalised

It is a deliberate error, produced on request, and it scores as correct work.

Everyone can nod at the 180 degree rule. Producing a violation on purpose, labelling it, and being able to point at what went wrong is the only evidence available that a student can *see* the rule rather than recite it. A strip 3 with a clean pair and no BROKEN panel loses the points, because the hard half is missing.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing.** Stick figures in correct positions beat beautiful figures in wrong ones, and this assignment is almost purely about positions.

**Story.** Six unrelated fragments is the correct answer. Connecting them narratively costs clarity and earns nothing.

---

## Where this goes

Single-stage, no cap. Placed after City Park deliberately: the rules arrive once as instruction and once as retrieval, several classes apart, which is how they stick.
