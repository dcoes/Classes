# Rubric — EX5: Continuity Strips
**40 points · Band EX · Assessed against CO1, CO2, CO3**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX5_Continuity_Strips.md`](../assignments/EX5_Continuity_Strips.md)

> CO1 — *Draw storyboards as a planning tool.* CO2 — *Describe basic film terminology.* CO3 — *Tabulate storyboards based on certain events.*

Six devices, six strips, twenty panels, on the 1.85 : 1 template. Demonstrating grammar on demand — which is the difference between having read about a device and being able to execute it.

---

| Component | Pts | CO | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|---|
| **Six strips, complete** | 6 | CO3 | Every strip present, every panel drawn. 1 pt per strip. |
| **Each device is the device** | 12 | CO2 | 2 pts per strip. Labels covered, a stranger names it. **Cut-in and cut-away must be distinguishable from each other.** |
| **Continuity holds inside each strip** | 8 | CO1 | Line held, screen direction consistent, eyelines answered — *within* the strip, which is where it's checkable. |
| **Floor plans — strips 1, 3, 6** | 6 | CO1 | Overhead diagram, cameras numbered, line marked, and it matches the panels. 2 pts each. |
| **The cutaway names its job** | 4 | CO3 | Strip 5 declares "buys time" or "gives the audience what the character doesn't have" — **and does it.** Naming one and executing the other scores half. |
| **Format and notation** | 4 | CO2 | 1.85 : 1 panels on the EX2 template, one strip per row read left to right, H1 notation, shot numbers by ten, strips labeled. |

**Total: 40**

---

## The per-strip checks

These are what "the device is the device" means. Each is worth 2 points, and each is objectively checkable.

| # | Device | It counts when |
|--:|---|---|
| 1 | Height reverse | Both cameras are on one side of the line, and screen positions **do not swap** between panels 2 and 3 |
| 2 | Match on action | Frame size changes noticeably **and** the cut lands mid-movement, not at the end of it |
| 3 | OTS shot/reverse | The pair is clean, **and** panel 4 is labeled BROKEN and actually crosses the line |
| 4 | Cut-in | The detail is **circled in the wide panel** — it was in the space already |
| 5 | Cut-away | Panel 2 is somewhere the main action isn't, and panel 3 shows time has passed |
| 6 | Cross-cutting | Four panels, A/B/A/B, converging on something |

---

## The distinction that carries the most weight

**Cut-in vs. cut-away vs. reaction shot.** These are three different tools and students routinely submit the same thing three times.

- A **cut-in** goes *closer into the same space*. The detail was always in the wide — which is why the circle on panel 1 is required, and why a strip without it cannot score full marks no matter how good the panels are.
- A **cut-away** *leaves* the space. Its job is to buy time or to hand the audience information the character doesn't have.
- A **reaction shot** stays inside the scene and shows someone responding. Useful, common, and **not either of the above.**

A student who submits a bystander watching the bus has drawn a reaction shot and labeled it a cutaway. That costs the 2 points on strip 5 and usually the 4 on naming the job, because a reaction shot cannot do either job the naming asks for.

---

## Why panel 4 of strip 3 is graded, not penalized

It is a deliberate error, produced on request. It scores as **correct work.**

Everyone can nod at the 180° rule. Producing a violation on purpose, labeling it, and being able to point at what went wrong is the only evidence available that a student can actually *see* the rule rather than recite it. A strip 3 with a clean OTS pair and no BROKEN panel loses the 2 points, because the hard half is missing.

---

## Common failures

| Failure | Cost |
|---|--:|
| Screen positions swap across the height reverse | 2 on strip 1, plus continuity points |
| Match cut drawn at the same frame size — a jump cut | 2 on strip 2 |
| BROKEN panel omitted, or drawn correctly by accident | 2 on strip 3 |
| Cut-in detail not circled in the wide | 2 on strip 4 |
| Cutaway is a reaction shot inside the scene | 2 on strip 5, usually 4 on the job |
| Cutaway job named but not executed — "buys time," no time passes | 2 of the 4 |
| Cross-cut strips that never converge | 2 on strip 6 |
| Floor plan drawn but contradicts the panels | Up to 6 |

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing.** Stick figures in correct positions outscore beautiful figures in wrong ones. This assignment is almost purely about positions.

**Story.** Six unrelated fragments is the correct answer. Connecting them narratively costs clarity and earns nothing.

---

## Milestone note

EX5 is single-stage and carries no milestone cap. It is placed **after** City Park deliberately — the rules arrive once as instruction (Session 4) and once as retrieval (Session 10), which is how they stick.
