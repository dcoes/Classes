# EX2 Template Build: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 4% of the course grade · Band EX**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX2_Template_Build.md`](../assignments/EX2_Template_Build.md)
**Assessed against CO1:** *Draw storyboards as a planning tool for their visual narratives.*

> Six templates: three ratios (1.33, 1.85, 2.39), each as an 8 x 4 working sheet and a 3 x 3 presentation sheet. Quarter-inch gutters, 2 inch margins. Nothing here is graded on drawing, because there isn't any.

---

## 1 . Three ratios, correct: 30%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 30 | All three ratios land within 1% of target, on both sheets. Measured, not approximated. |
| Good | 26 | Two of three exact. The third is off by more than 1%, but the working shows an arithmetic slip rather than a guess. |
| Average | 21 | One ratio correct. The other two are close but unmeasured. |
| Developing | 15 | Ratios eyeballed throughout, or width and height swapped on one sheet. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted, or no recognisable ratio work. |

**Tolerance.** A panel passes if `width / height` lands within 1% of target.

| Ratio | Acceptable range |
|---|---|
| 1.33 : 1 | 1.317 to 1.343 |
| 1.85 : 1 | 1.832 to 1.869 |
| 2.39 : 1 | 2.366 to 2.414 |

A panel built at 2.35 fails the 2.39 line. The gap is 1.7%, which is outside tolerance. Both numbers are real and they are seventeen years apart. Only one of them is this assignment.

## 2 . Grid geometry: 20%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 20 | 8 columns x 4 rows, quarter-inch gutters, 2 inch margins, panels aligned to guides. Project name flush left and student name flush right, both a quarter inch above the first row. |
| Good | 17 | Grid and margins correct. Name blocks misplaced or missing. |
| Average | 14 | Grid correct, but gutters or margins are inconsistent across the three sheets. |
| Developing | 10 | Panels placed by eye. No guides in the file. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 3 . The arithmetic, shown: 15%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 15 | Panel width, panel height, and the row check are worked out and visible for every ratio and both layouts. |
| Good | 13 | Working shown for one layout, applied silently to the other. |
| Average | 11 | Some working shown. The row check is missing. |
| Developing | 8 | A single calculation, or numbers with no derivation. |
| Missing | 0 | No arithmetic anywhere. |

**This is where partial credit lives.** An arithmetic slip with visible working scores well. A guess offers nothing to give partial credit to, which is the whole reason the working is a graded line rather than a suggestion.

## 4 . The presentation sheet, derived: 15%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 15 | 3 x 3 sheet built for all three ratios, from the same formula, with panel width correctly recomputed. |
| Good | 13 | All three built. One carries a derivation error. |
| Average | 11 | Built for one or two ratios only. |
| Developing | 8 | A larger sheet exists but was sized by eye rather than derived. |
| Missing | 0 | No presentation sheet. |

**Why this line exists.** The 8 x 4 grid yields panels two thirds of an inch wide on letter paper. That is a thumbnail, not a board. Recognising it by measuring, and then deriving a sheet you can actually draw in, is the reasoning this assignment tests. It also gives thumbnails and finished boards two physically different homes, which matters on the final project.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing.** There is none in this assignment.

**Decoration.** A designed header does not make a better template.

---

## Where this goes

EX2 is single-stage and carries no milestone cap, but almost everything downstream is delivered on these files. EX3, EX4, EX5, PR1 and PR4 all use them, and the working/presentation split is what keeps thumbnails from being promoted into final art on the final project.

Boards submitted on a template that was never graded are boards on an unverified ratio, and they get assessed as such.
