# PR5 The Pitch: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 5% of the course grade · Band PR**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR5_The_Pitch.md`](../assignments/PR5_The_Pitch.md)
**Assessed against CO5:** *Explain the creative work of self and peers.*

**Format:** 3 to 4 minutes, live, in front of your boards, projected or pinned.
**What you pitch:** your final sequence, in whatever state it's in. Incomplete is expected and fine.
**Deliverable:** the live pitch, plus your posted notes within 24 hours. A recorded version is accepted for a second attempt, or from anyone genuinely unable to present live.

---

## 1 . Setup: 10%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 10 | Establishes where we are, who we're with and what just happened, in one or two sentences, then moves. |
| Good | 8 | Set up clearly, slightly long. |
| Average | 7 | Set up, but the room needs a question answered before the sequence starts. |
| Developing | 5 | Begins mid-sequence with no orientation, or spends a minute on preamble. |
| Missing | 0 | No pitch delivered. |

One sentence, then start. Everything between that sentence and the sequence is stalling.

## 2 . Performance of the sequence: 30%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 30 | Present tense, active, performed rather than described. Voices attempted. The room follows without reading captions. |
| Good | 26 | Performed throughout, voices skipped or half-committed. |
| Average | 21 | Largely described rather than performed. The room can follow. |
| Developing | 15 | Reading captions aloud, or reading from notes. |
| Missing | 0 | No pitch delivered. |

*"She turns. She sees it. She runs."* Not *"in this shot we see a character who is turning."*

**Doing the voices badly is fine. Doing them silently is not.** You will feel ridiculous. Everyone feels ridiculous, and the room still needs to hear the dialogue timed against the cut.

## 3 . Timing: 20%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 20 | The pacing of the pitch reflects the pacing of the sequence. Holds are held, fast cuts are fast, the word "hold" appears on a hold. |
| Good | 17 | Mostly reflects the cut, with a few passages delivered at a uniform pace. |
| Average | 14 | Even delivery throughout regardless of what the sequence does. |
| Developing | 10 | Rushed or padded to fill the time. |
| Missing | 0 | No pitch delivered. |

**This is what separates a pitch from a description.** You are performing the edit, not summarising it.

## 4 . The one thing: 20%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 20 | Identifies what the sequence is *about* and lands that moment deliberately. |
| Good | 17 | Knows what it's about, lands it without much emphasis. |
| Average | 14 | Can name it when asked, but the pitch doesn't build toward it. |
| Developing | 10 | Every beat delivered at equal weight. |
| Missing | 0 | No pitch delivered. |

Every sequence has one moment it exists for. If you don't know which one, the room will decide for you, and they will be wrong.

## 5 . Taking notes: 20%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 20 | Receives at least two notes without defending. Asks at least one clarifying question about intent. Writes them down, and posts them within 24 hours with one line on what changes. |
| Good | 17 | Takes notes well, no clarifying question, follow-up posted. |
| Average | 14 | Takes notes, explains the work once or twice while they're being given. |
| Developing | 10 | Defends throughout, or no follow-up posted. |
| Missing | 0 | Absent from the session. |

The response to a note is *"got it"*, or a clarifying question. Not an explanation of what you meant.

This is hard, it feels unnatural, and it is the most professionally valuable 20% in this course. **If you have to explain what you meant, the boards didn't say it, and that is the note.**

Disagreeing is allowed and costs nothing. *"Let me try both"* and *"can I show you why I went the other way"* are professional answers. Being precious is what costs.

---

## Not graded

**Nervousness. Polish of speech. Accent. Drawing quality of the boards being pitched.**

> **Nerves are not a deduction. Defending your work instead of hearing the note is.**

**Business framing.** Market, platform, audience size, comparable titles, profitability: none of it is assessed here, and preparing it is preparing the wrong thing. That is the *business* pitch, and Game Design owns it. A business pitch sells the idea. **A story pitch makes the room see the film.**

---

## Peer notes count for the note-giver

Notes come from the instructor and from three assigned peers, rotating. **Giving notes is assessed.** For the student giving them, a substantive note is CO5 evidence in its own right, and it is the reason a student cannot meet CO5 by pitching well and sitting silently through everyone else's turn.

---

## Why this assignment exists

Pitching is the part of the job that survives everything. It requires zero drawing ability, it is how boards actually move through a studio, and for an independent artist it is the difference between having work and having a career.

It is also the one assessment in this course where a student with brilliant instincts and shaky hands can be visibly, unambiguously excellent, and the strongest available evidence for CO5, which was otherwise the least-assessed objective on the books.
