# PR5 The Pitch: Rubric
**50 points · Band PR · Assessed against CO5**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR5_The_Pitch.md`](../assignments/PR5_The_Pitch.md)

> CO5: *Explain the creative work of self and peers.*

**Format:** 3–4 minutes, live, in front of your boards: projected or pinned. Assigned Session 9; pitches run Sessions 14–15, scheduled.

**What you pitch:** your final project sequence, in whatever state it's in. Incomplete is expected and fine: pitch what exists and describe what's coming.

**Deliverable:** the live pitch, plus your posted notes within 24 hours. A recorded version is accepted for a second attempt, or from anyone genuinely unable to present live.

---

| Component | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| **Setup** | 5 | Establishes where, who, and what just happened in one or two sentences, then moves. |
| **Performance of the sequence** | 15 | Present tense, active, performed rather than described. Voices attempted. The room can follow without reading captions. |
| **Timing** | 10 | The pacing of the pitch reflects the pacing of the sequence. Holds are held; fast cuts are fast. |
| **The one thing** | 10 | Identifies what the sequence is *about* and lands that moment deliberately. |
| **Taking notes** | 10 | Receives at least two notes without defending. Asks at least one clarifying question about intent. Writes them down, and posts them with one line on what changes. |

**Total: 50**

---

## Not graded

**Nervousness. Polish of speech. Accent. Drawing quality of the boards being pitched.**

> **Nerves are not a deduction. Defending your work instead of hearing the note is.**

---

## Peer notes count for the note-giver

Notes come from the instructor and from three assigned peers, rotating. **Giving notes is assessed.** For the student giving them, a substantive note is CO5 evidence in its own right. It is recorded, and it is the reason a student cannot meet CO5 by pitching well and sitting silently through everyone else's turn.

---

## What the "taking notes" line is really testing

The response to a note is *"got it,"* or a clarifying question. Not an explanation of what you meant.

This is hard, it feels unnatural, and it is the most professionally valuable ten points in the course. If a student has to explain what they meant, the boards didn't say it, **and that is the note.**

Disagreeing is allowed and costs nothing: *"let me try both"* and *"can I show you why I went the other way"* are professional answers. Being precious is what costs.

---

## The boundary with Game Design

CA 140 assesses the **sequence pitch**: performing boards aloud, doing the voices, pitching the timing, standing beside the wall and making the room see the film. The *Snow White* precedent, Disney pinning boards to a wall and acting the picture out for his crew, belongs to this course, and it is literally the origin of the storyboard format.

Game Design owns the **business pitch**: market, platform, profitability, comparable titles, the client's point of view. **None of that is assessed here.** A student who arrives with a market analysis has prepared the wrong thing and should not be scored on it in either direction.

A business pitch sells the idea. A story pitch makes the room see the film.

---

## Why this assignment exists

Pitching is the part of the job that survives everything. It requires zero drawing ability, it is how boards actually move through a studio, and for an independent artist it is the difference between having work and having a career.

It is also the one assessment in this course where a student with brilliant instincts and shaky hands can be visibly, unambiguously excellent, and the strongest available evidence for CO5, which was otherwise the least-assessed objective on the books.
