# Rubric — EX3: Twelve Shots
**30 points · Band EX · Assessed against CO1, CO2**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX3_Twelve_Shots.md`](../assignments/EX3_Twelve_Shots.md)

> CO1 — *Draw storyboards as a planning tool.* CO2 — *Describe basic film terminology.*

Twelve shots, thirteen panels, one subject, one location. The only variable is the camera.

---

| Component | Pts | CO | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|---|
| **Twelve shots present and correctly named** | 12 | CO2 | 1 pt each. The label under the panel matches what the panel shows. |
| **The required eight are unambiguously that shot** | 8 | CO1, CO2 | 1 pt each. A stranger with H2 in hand identifies each without the label. |
| **Captions say what the shot does** | 6 | CO2 | The caption states purpose, not position. A caption that describes the camera scores zero for that panel. |
| **Panel 13 — the wrong shot** | 2 | CO2 | A genuinely wrong choice for the moment, with one line saying why. |
| **Subject held constant, format correct** | 2 | CO1 | One subject, one location, all thirteen. 1.85 : 1 on the EX2 template, H1 notation. |

**Total: 30**

---

## The captions line, in detail

This is the six points students most often lose without understanding why, so grade it visibly and say it out loud when the assignment is issued.

| Caption | Score |
|---|---|
| *"Low angle. Camera is below her eyeline."* | 0 — describes the camera |
| *"Low angle, makes her look powerful."* | Partial — a purpose, but generic and unattached to the moment |
| *"Low angle — she has the power in this room now."* | Full — a purpose tied to *this* subject in *this* moment |

The label is vocabulary. The caption is judgment. A submission with twelve perfect labels and twelve positional captions has demonstrated a student can read a glossary, which is not what CO2 is for.

Grade the six as a whole, not per panel: full 6 where most captions state purpose, 3 where it's mixed, 0 where the pattern is positional throughout.

---

## What "unambiguously that shot" means

For the required eight, cover the label. Someone holding H2 should name it.

| Shot | It counts when |
|---|---|
| **XLS** | The subject is small in a large space. Scale is the content. |
| **MS** | Roughly waist-up. Not a full figure, not a bust. |
| **ECU** | Tighter than a face — an eye, a hand, a detail. A head filling the frame is a CU. |
| **Low angle** | **The horizon line has moved.** See below. |
| **High angle** | Same, in the other direction. |
| **Dutch** | The horizon is canted. A tilted *subject* is not a Dutch angle. |
| **OTS** | A shoulder or head occupies the near foreground, in focus terms "in the way." |
| **POV** | The viewing character is **not in the frame.** |

---

## The failure that costs the most

**Low and high angles drawn as tilted heads rather than moved cameras.**

If the low angle and the eye-level shot share a horizon line, the camera never moved — the student drew a face looking up. That is a miss on the required-eight line for both panels.

It is worth marking clearly rather than gently, because Session 6 (Perspective) opens on exactly this and the deck's best line is *"the horizon line is your camera height."* A student who has just lost points on it arrives at that slide ready to hear it. The sequencing is deliberate: the problem lands two sessions before the tool.

Second most common: **POV with the viewing character in frame** — the same error EX1 catches on day two, reappearing. If a student misses it twice, it's worth a direct conversation rather than another deduction.

---

## Common failures

| Failure | Cost |
|---|--:|
| Twelve unrelated subjects instead of one | 2 on format, plus most of the required-eight line becomes unassessable |
| Captions describe the camera | Up to 6 |
| Low/high angle with an unmoved horizon | 2 |
| Dutch angle achieved by tilting the subject | 1 |
| POV includes the viewing character | 1, plus the equivalent on the naming line |
| Location chosen with no second person — OTS and POV impossible | 2 |
| Panel 13 omitted | 2 |

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing.** A stick figure in an empty room is a full-credit subject. What's assessed is whether the *camera* is where the label says.

**Ambition.** Twelve shots of someone waiting at a bus stop is a correct and complete answer.

---

## Milestone note

Single-stage, no cap. It is direct preparation for **PR2**, where the same skill — naming a shot and saying what it does — is applied at length to someone else's sequence. Students who lose the caption points here and don't correct course lose them again on a hundred-point assignment.
