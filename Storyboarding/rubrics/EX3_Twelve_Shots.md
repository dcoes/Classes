# EX3 Twelve Shots: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 4% of the course grade · Band EX**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX3_Twelve_Shots.md`](../assignments/EX3_Twelve_Shots.md)
**Assessed against CO1** (*storyboards as a planning tool*) **and CO2** (*basic film terminology*).

> Twelve shots, thirteen panels, one subject, one location. The only variable is the camera.

---

## 1 . Twelve shots present and correctly named: 30%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 30 | All twelve panels present. Every label matches what the panel shows. |
| Good | 26 | Twelve present, one or two labels wrong. |
| Average | 21 | Ten or eleven panels, labels mostly correct. |
| Developing | 15 | Eight or nine panels, or labels applied loosely. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 2 . The required eight are unambiguously that shot: 25%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 25 | Labels covered, a stranger holding H2 names all eight: XLS, MS, ECU, low angle, high angle, Dutch, OTS, POV. |
| Good | 21 | Six or seven read cleanly. |
| Average | 18 | Four or five read cleanly. |
| Developing | 13 | Two or three read cleanly. |
| Missing | 0 | None identifiable, or the required eight aren't attempted. |

**What "unambiguously" means:**

| Shot | It counts when |
|---|---|
| **XLS** | The subject is small in a large space. Scale is the content. |
| **MS** | Roughly waist up. Not a full figure, not a bust. |
| **ECU** | Tighter than a face: an eye, a hand, a detail. A head filling the frame is a CU. |
| **Low angle** | **The horizon line has moved.** |
| **High angle** | Same, in the other direction. |
| **Dutch** | The horizon is canted. A tilted *subject* is not a Dutch angle. |
| **OTS** | A shoulder or head occupies the near foreground. |
| **POV** | The viewing character is **not in the frame.** |

## 3 . Captions say what the shot does: 25%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 25 | Captions state purpose, tied to this subject in this moment. *"Low angle, she has the power in this room now."* |
| Good | 21 | Purpose stated, but generic and unattached to the moment. *"Low angle, makes her look powerful."* |
| Average | 18 | Mixed. Some purpose, some position. |
| Developing | 13 | Captions describe the camera throughout. *"Low angle. Camera is below her eyeline."* |
| Missing | 0 | No captions. |

**This is the line students most often lose without understanding why**, so it's worth naming when the assignment is issued. The label is vocabulary. The caption is judgment. Twelve perfect labels with twelve positional captions demonstrates a student can read a glossary, which is not what CO2 is for.

## 4 . Panel 13, the wrong shot: 10%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 10 | A genuinely wrong choice for the moment, with one line saying why it's wrong. |
| Good | 8 | Present, reasoning thin. |
| Average | 7 | Present, no reasoning. |
| Developing | 5 | An unremarkable shot labelled "wrong" with no argument. |
| Missing | 0 | Omitted. |

This is the first time in the course a student defends a choice, and arguing against a bad shot is easier than arguing for a good one. That's why it's here and not later.

## 5 . Subject held constant, and format: 10%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 10 | One subject, one location, all thirteen panels. On the EX2 presentation sheet, H1 notation. |
| Good | 8 | Constant subject, format slips. |
| Average | 7 | Subject changes once or twice. |
| Developing | 5 | Twelve unrelated subjects. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**The constraint is the assignment.** Twelve unrelated drawings teach nothing, because when everything changes at once you can't tell what any one change did. Hold the subject and the only variable left is the camera.

---

## The failure that costs the most

**Low and high angles drawn as tilted heads rather than moved cameras.** If the low angle and the eye-level shot share a horizon line, the camera never moved and the student drew a face looking up.

Mark it clearly rather than gently. The perspective class opens on exactly this, and its best line is *"the horizon line is your camera height."* A student who has just lost points on it arrives ready to hear that. The sequencing is deliberate: the problem lands before the tool.

Second most common: **POV with the viewing character in frame**, which is the same error EX1 catches in week one.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing.** A stick figure in an empty room is a full-credit subject. What's assessed is whether the *camera* is where the label says.

**Ambition.** Twelve shots of someone waiting at a bus stop is a correct and complete answer.

---

## Where this goes

Direct preparation for **PR2**, where the same skill (naming a shot and saying what it does) gets applied at length to someone else's sequence. Students who lose the caption points here and don't correct course lose them again on the midterm.
