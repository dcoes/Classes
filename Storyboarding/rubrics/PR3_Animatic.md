# PR3 Animatic: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 6% of the course grade · Band PR**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR3_Animatic.md`](../assignments/PR3_Animatic.md)
**Assessed against CO1** (*planning tool*), **CO3** (*tabulating from events*) **and CO4** (*visually aesthetic images*).

> Boards handle blocking, camera and action. They cannot tell you **how long.** This assignment assesses the one dimension a board can't carry.
>
> An animatic is not a prototype of the film. It's a prototype of the **film experience**, which is why timing and temp audio matter more here than drawing does.

**Milestone chain:** panel prep, rough cut, sound, final animatic. **Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at half its available percentage.**

---

## 1 . Panel prep and file discipline: 15%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 15 | Correct ratio and resolution, 2x where a push or pan is planned. `SEQ01_SH010.png` naming, incrementing by ten. Moving elements on their own layers with the hole behind filled. Both `.psd` and `.png` delivered. |
| Good | 13 | All present, numbering not by ten. |
| Average | 11 | Files usable, layer separation incomplete, so some planned moves are impossible. |
| Developing | 8 | One flat page per panel, no separation, inconsistent naming. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**Sloppy file prep is not a minor sin here.** It is what makes the next three stages take four times as long, which is exactly what it does on a real production, at four times the cost.

## 2 . Rough cut, coverage and frame rate: 12%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 12 | The full sequence is cut, no placeholder gaps. One frame rate chosen at the start and never changed. |
| Good | 10 | Complete, with one or two gaps still holding. |
| Average | 8 | Most of the sequence cut. |
| Developing | 6 | A partial cut, or the frame rate changed mid-project. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 3 . Holds are decisions: 8%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 8 | Durations reflect the H5 reference or deviate from it deliberately. Establishing shots breathe. Inserts don't linger. |
| Good | 7 | Mostly considered, with two or three arbitrary holds. |
| Average | 6 | A default duration applied broadly, with some variation. |
| Developing | 4 | Every panel the same length. |
| Missing | 0 | Not assessable. |

## 4 . Sound: 15%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 15 | Music or ambience under the whole sequence. Scratch dialogue recorded wherever there is dialogue. Sources credited and legally licensed. |
| Good | 13 | All present, sources not credited. |
| Average | 11 | Music present, dialogue silent. |
| Developing | 8 | A single track dropped underneath with no relationship to the cut. |
| Missing | 0 | Silent. |

**Scratch dialogue recorded on a phone, badly, is fine. Silent is not.** Timing needs a voice to time against.

## 5 . Timing across the sequence: 20%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 20 | Pacing reads end to end. Nothing drags, nothing is confusing from being too short. The rhythm changes where the story changes. |
| Good | 17 | Reads throughout, with one section that sits wrong. |
| Average | 14 | Broadly workable, with several passages that drag or rush. |
| Developing | 10 | The sequence plays but the pacing is uniform and the shape is lost. |
| Missing | 0 | Not assessable. |

The heaviest single line, deliberately. This is the dimension the boards could not carry, and it is the reason the assignment exists.

## 6 . Panel readability in motion: 10%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 10 | Panels read at speed. Figure and ground hold. Nothing that mattered was too small to see at its actual duration. |
| Good | 8 | Reads throughout, one or two panels need longer than they get. |
| Average | 7 | Several panels don't resolve at the speed they're cut. |
| Developing | 5 | Most panels are illegible in motion. |
| Missing | 0 | Not assessable. |

## 7 . Camera moves executed: 8%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 8 | Pans and pushes work, because the artwork was drawn wide or large enough to support them. |
| Good | 7 | Moves work, with one that reaches the edge of its artwork. |
| Average | 6 | Moves attempted, softness or edges visible. |
| Developing | 4 | Moves planned but not achievable from the artwork supplied. |
| Missing | 0 | No moves where the boards called for them. |

## 8 . Evidence of iteration: 7%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 7 | At least three timing passes, demonstrable through versioned files or a written note on what changed between them and why. |
| Good | 6 | Three passes, evidence thin. |
| Average | 5 | Two passes. |
| Developing | 3 | One pass, asserted as final. |
| Missing | 0 | No evidence. |

Your first timing pass will be wrong. Everyone's is.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing quality.** Barely relevant here, and the assignment is structured to prove it. A crude animatic with excellent timing is a good animatic. A beautiful one with bad timing is not.

**Effects, transitions, polish.** Cuts are over 90% of transitions in finished films and should be about 90% of yours. A dissolve you can't justify costs more than it earns.

---

## The failure this assignment exists to catch

Students time from memory, because they believe they already know how long things take.

They don't. Neither do you. **Every animator should own a stopwatch and most never use one.**

Both timing failures, too long and too short, look identical in a submission. They both look like "bad animatic," and they have **opposite fixes.** Learn to tell them apart before you submit, not after.
