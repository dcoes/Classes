# PR2 Midterm, Reverse Analysis: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 25% of the course grade · Band PR**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR2_Midterm_Reverse_Analysis.md`](../assignments/PR2_Midterm_Reverse_Analysis.md)
**Assessed against CO2** (*film terminology*), **CO3** (*tabulating storyboards from events*) **and CO5** (*explaining creative work*).

> You take a finished sequence and board it backwards, from the screen to the page, then explain what the director did and why.
>
> This is the one assignment where you invent nothing. Everything you need is on the screen. The test is whether you can *see* it.

**Deliverables:** a shot log, boards reconstructed from observation, a written analysis of 500 to 800 words, and substantive contributions to two other students' analyses in the critique class.

---

## 1 . Shot log, the tabulation: 25%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 25 | Every shot logged in order with shot size, angle, camera movement, and approximate duration. Count accurate. Nothing merged, nothing missed. |
| Good | 21 | Complete and in order, with durations estimated rather than timed. |
| Average | 18 | Most shots logged. A few cuts merged into one entry. |
| Developing | 13 | Partial log, or one attribute missing throughout. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**Merged cuts are the tell.** A student who logs eighteen shots in a twenty-six shot sequence did not watch it with a finger on the pause key, and it is visible without checking their work against the film.

## 2 . Boards from observation: 35%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 35 | Panels reconstruct the staging accurately: framing, character position, screen direction and eyelines match what's on screen. Camera moves notated per H1. |
| Good | 30 | Staging accurate throughout, notation inconsistent. |
| Average | 25 | Framing broadly right, screen direction or eyelines drift from the source in places. |
| Developing | 18 | Panels present but only loosely related to the sequence. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

Drawing quality is not assessed here. **Stick figures in the correct positions outscore beautiful figures in the wrong ones**, every time, because the thing being measured is whether the student could see the staging.

## 3 . Written analysis: 30%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 30 | Terminology correct and specific. At least three deliberate choices identified, each with an argument about what it *does*. Structure named. At least one broken convention identified and explained. |
| Good | 26 | All elements present, with one argument that asserts rather than supports. |
| Average | 21 | Choices identified but described rather than argued. Structure named loosely. |
| Developing | 15 | Largely a plot summary with terminology attached. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**Not a summary. The plot is not the analysis.**

**Strong:** *"The sequence holds a single line of action for the first nine shots, then crosses it deliberately on the reveal, so the disorientation lands on the audience at the same moment it lands on the character."*

**Weak:** *"The director uses many different camera angles to make the scene interesting."*

The first names a decision and says what the decision does. Every paragraph should survive the question *"and so what?"*

## 4 . Peer discussion: 10%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 10 | Substantive, specific observations on at least two other students' analyses. Notes describe rather than evaluate. |
| Good | 8 | Two contributions, one of them general. |
| Average | 7 | One substantive contribution. |
| Developing | 5 | Present and largely silent. |
| Missing | 0 | Absent. |

This is CO5 evidence and it cannot be met by submitting work alone.

---

## Terminology is graded strictly

This is the CO2 assessment, and vocabulary is the one thing in this course with objectively right answers.

- "It zooms in" when the camera **trucked** is wrong, and costs points.
- "A medium shot" for something clearly a medium close-up is imprecise, and costs fewer.
- "It's a cool shot" is not terminology.

**H2 is open-book.** There is no reason to lose these marks.

---

## No milestone cap, one hard prerequisite

The midterm is a single submission with no cascading cap. It does have a prerequisite that cannot be faked: **you cannot log shots you haven't watched.**

Three passes. Once for the story, with no notes. Once for the shots, pausing constantly. Once with a stopwatch for durations. It is obvious in a submission which students did this.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing quality of the reconstruction.** These are observation boards.

**Agreeing with the director.** You may argue a choice was a mistake. A specific, supported argument scores exactly the same as praise. What doesn't score is an unsupported opinion in either direction.
