# Rubric — PR2: Midterm, Reverse Analysis
**100 points · Band PR · Assessed against CO2, CO3, CO5**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR2_Midterm_Reverse_Analysis.md`](../assignments/PR2_Midterm_Reverse_Analysis.md)

> You are given a finished sequence. You board it backwards — from the screen to the page — and then explain what the director did and why.
>
> This is the one assignment where you are not inventing anything. Everything you need is on the screen. The test is whether you can *see* it.

---

## Deliverables

1. A **shot log** of the assigned sequence
2. **Boards** reconstructed from observation
3. A **written analysis**, 500–800 words
4. **Peer discussion** in the critique session

---

| Component | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| **Shot log / tabulation** *(CO3)* | 25 | Every shot in the sequence logged in order with: shot size, angle, any camera movement, and approximate duration. Count is accurate. Nothing merged, nothing missed. |
| **Boards from observation** *(CO2, CO3)* | 35 | Panels reconstruct the sequence's staging accurately — framing, character position, screen direction, and eyelines match what's on screen. Camera moves notated correctly per H1. |
| **Written analysis** *(CO2, CO5)* | 30 | Uses shot terminology correctly and specifically. Identifies at least three deliberate choices and argues what each one does. Names the sequence's structure. Explains at least one moment where the director broke a convention and why it works. |
| **Peer discussion** *(CO5)* | 10 | Contributes substantive observations to at least two other students' analyses in the critique session. Notes are specific, not evaluative. |

**Total: 100**

---

## What "correct terminology" means here

This is the CO2 assessment — *describe basic film terminology* — and it's graded strictly, because vocabulary is the one thing in this course with objectively right answers.

- "It zooms in" when the camera trucked is **wrong** and costs points.
- "A medium shot" for a shot that is clearly a medium close-up is **imprecise** and costs fewer.
- "It's a cool shot" is not terminology.

H2 is the reference list. It is open-book. There is no reason to lose these points.

---

## What the written analysis is looking for

Not a summary. The plot is not the analysis.

**Strong:** *"The sequence holds a single line of action for the first nine shots, then crosses it deliberately on the reveal — the disorientation lands on the audience at the same moment it lands on the character."*

**Weak:** *"The director uses many different camera angles to make the scene interesting."*

The difference is that the first one names a decision and says what the decision does. Every paragraph should be able to survive the question *"and so what?"*

---

## No milestone cap

The midterm is a single submission and carries no cascading cap. It does have a hard prerequisite: **you cannot log shots you haven't watched.** Watch the sequence at least three times — once for the story, once for the shots, once with a stopwatch.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing quality of the reconstruction.** These are observation boards. Stick figures with correct staging outscore beautiful figures in the wrong positions, every time.

**Agreeing with the director.** You may argue that a choice was a mistake. If the argument is specific and supported, it scores the same as praise.
