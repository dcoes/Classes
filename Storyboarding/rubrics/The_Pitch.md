# Rubric — The Pitch
**50 points · Assessed against CO5**

> CO5 — *Explain the creative work of self and peers.*
>
> This rubric is lifted verbatim from `NewLectures/decks-new-09-10-11.md`. The deck and the rubric say the same words on purpose.

---

**Format:** 3–4 minutes, live, in front of your boards (projected or pinned). Session 14 or 15, scheduled.

**What you pitch:** your final project sequence, in whatever state it's in. Incomplete is expected and fine — pitch what exists and describe what's coming.

**Deliverable:** the live pitch. Optionally, a recorded version submitted to Canvas for students who want a second attempt or who are genuinely unable to present live.

---

## Rubric

| Component | Pts | Full credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Setup** | 5 | Establishes where, who, and what just happened in one or two sentences, then moves |
| **Performance of the sequence** | 15 | Present tense, active, performed rather than described; voices attempted; the room can follow without reading captions |
| **Timing** | 10 | Pacing of the pitch reflects the pacing of the sequence; holds are held, fast cuts are fast |
| **The one thing** | 10 | Can identify what the sequence is *about* and lands that moment deliberately |
| **Taking notes** | 10 | Receives at least two notes without defending; asks at least one clarifying question about intent; writes them down |

**Total: 50**

**Not graded:** nervousness, polish of speech, accent, drawing quality of the boards being pitched.

> **Nerves are not a deduction. Defending your work instead of hearing the note is.**

---

## Running it in class

- **Schedule it** — 3 to 4 min pitch plus 2 min of notes is roughly 6 min per student. For 20 students that's two full sessions; for 12 it's one plus change.
- **Notes come from you AND from three assigned peers, rotating.** Peers giving notes get process credit for it — this is CO5 evidence for the note-giver as well as the pitcher.
- **Establish the rule out loud before the first pitch:** the artist does not respond during notes except to ask a clarifying question. They can respond after all notes are given.
- **Follow-up milestone:** within 24 hours, each student posts the notes they received and one line on what they'll change. That converts the pitch from a performance into a revision mechanism.

## The boundary with Game Design

CA 140 owns the **sequence pitch**: performing boards aloud, doing the voices, pitching the timing, standing beside the wall and making the room see the film. The *Snow White* precedent — Disney pinning boards to a wall and acting the picture out for his crew — is this course's, and it is literally the origin of the storyboard format.

Game Design owns the **business pitch**: market, platform, profitability, comparable titles, the client's point of view. **None of that is assessed here and none of it should appear in this rubric or in the brief.**

Say the distinction out loud in class: *a business pitch sells the idea; a story pitch makes the room see the film.* Students who have taken both courses should hear that this one is a specialization of a skill they already have a foundation in.

One line worth importing from that course verbatim, because it's the right answer to student nerves:

> *"Because as the designer, no one knows more about the project than you do."*

The same is true of the board artist.

---

## Why this assignment exists

Pitching is the part of the job that survives everything. It requires zero drawing ability, it's how boards actually move through a studio, and for an independent artist it's the difference between having work and having a career.

It is also the one skill in this course where a student with brilliant instincts and shaky hands can be visibly, unambiguously excellent — and the strongest available evidence for CO5, which was otherwise the least-assessed objective in the course.
