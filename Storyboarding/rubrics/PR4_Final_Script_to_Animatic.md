# Rubric — PR4: Final, Script to Animatic
**200 points · Band PR · Assessed against CO1, CO2, CO3, CO4, CO5**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR4_Final_Script_to_Animatic.md`](../assignments/PR4_Final_Script_to_Animatic.md)

> Everything, end to end. A script becomes a beat sheet becomes thumbnails becomes boards becomes a timed animatic with sound.
>
> The opening question on the brief is the one that matters: **what experience do you want the audience to have?** Answer it before you draw anything, and every decision downstream gets easier.

> **Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at 50% of that stage's available points.**
>
> Across seven stages the cascade is severe by design. A student who skips thumbnails and roughs to arrive with finished boards loses 55 points outright and has their 45-point final boards capped at 22.5 — a 77.5-point penalty on a 200-point assignment. **Skipping process is not a shortcut here; it is a failing grade.**

## 1 · Script markup + beat sheet — 25 pts · CO2, CO3

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| **Script markup** | 10 | The script is highlighted by shot size, one colour per size, with margin annotations for angle, framing and camera move. Terminology correct *(CO2)*. |
| Events tabulated in order | 8 | Every beat, one line each. |
| Beat → panel mapping | 4 | Intended panel count per beat. |
| Structure identified | 3 | The sequence's shape is named — where it turns, what the hook is, what the sequence is *about*. |

*The markup is the cheapest diagnostic in the whole chain, for the student and for the grader. **A long unbroken run of one colour is coverage that never varies. A colour change on every line is cutting for its own sake.** Both are visible at a glance, on a page, before a single panel exists.*

## 2 · Shot list + floor plans — 20 pts · CO2, CO3

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| Shot list complete | 10 | Framing, angle, and script reference for every intended shot. Terminology correct *(CO2)*. |
| Overhead diagrams | 10 | Floor plan for every location, with camera positions numbered and the line of action marked. Catches crossed lines before a panel is drawn. |

*The floor plan is the most underused tool in the discipline. Nobody makes you do it. Everybody who does it has fewer problems. Here, someone is making you do it.*

## 3 · Thumbnails — 25 pts · CO1

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| **Coverage — the sprint sheet** | 8 | The in-class Session 12 sprint sheet, every beat present, photographed and posted the same session. Nothing rendered. |
| **Divergence** | 9 | Five designated beats, three ways each. **A variant counts only if it changes shot size, camera angle, blocking, or which character the frame is on.** |
| **The rejected one** | 6 | Choice marked per beat, plus one line on what the rejected version did better. |
| **Format discipline** | 2 | Delivered on the EX2 working sheet. Oversize panels score zero for that beat. |

> **A final panel that is a scan, trace, or upscale of a thumbnail scores as a missing panel** on stage 5.

*This stage grades exploration, not drawings. Every line is countable — a sheet, a count of variants, a count of marked choices, a page measurement — which is what makes it possible to grade brainstorming without grading taste.*

*The sprint sheet is made in class on purpose. Ninety seconds a beat is too fast to render, the work is demonstrably the student's own, and it puts the first pass in a room the instructor is standing in. That single mechanism addresses grading brainstorming, provenance, and work-session attendance at once.*

## 4 · Rough boards — 30 pts · CO1, CO4

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| Action and dialogue blocked | 10 | Legible in every panel. |
| Continuity established | 11 | Line held, screen direction consistent, eyelines matched, action matched across cuts. |
| Silhouette readability | 9 | Poses read filled solid black. |

## 5 · Final boards — 45 pts · CO1, CO2, CO4

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| **Continuity across the whole sequence** | 12 | Holds from first panel to last. Deliberate breaks are earned and identifiable as deliberate. |
| **Composition** | 12 | Figure/ground, one dominant element, headroom/lookroom/leadroom, contrast across the cut. Three planes where depth matters. |
| **Staging and acting** | 9 | Every character wants something in every panel. Line of action present. Weight believable. Listener staged where the reaction is the shot. |
| **Notation and terminology** | 7 | H1 conventions throughout. Shot sizes and angles named correctly. *(CO2)* |
| **Document completeness** | 5 | Correct ratio, panel and shot numbers matched to the script's scene numbers, action described beneath each panel. |

## 6 · Animatic — 45 pts · CO3, CO4

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| Timing | 20 | Pacing reads across the whole piece. Rhythm changes where the story changes. |
| Sound | 10 | Temp music or ambience throughout, scratch dialogue where there's dialogue, sources credited and legally licensed. |
| Camera moves executed | 8 | Pans and pushes supported by artwork drawn wide or large enough. |
| Delivery and iteration | 7 | Correct ratio and frame rate, plays cleanly, at least three timing passes evidenced. |

## 7 · Critique participation — 10 pts · CO5

| | Pts | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|
| Notes given to peers | 6 | Substantive, specific notes to at least three other students across the final critique sessions. |
| Notes received and acted on | 4 | Documented: what you were told, and one line on what you changed. Disagreeing is allowed; ignoring is not. |

## The pitch gate

Every student receives an assigned script for this project. **Nobody is stranded.**

A student may instead **pitch their own premise** to replace the assigned script. The pitch happens in Session 14, before the final is finished, which means it also functions as a milestone that catches problems while there's still time to fix them.

**The gate is real. A pitch can be declined.** A declined pitch costs nothing but the swap — you proceed on the assigned script with no penalty. A gate that always opens teaches nothing.

The Pitch is separately graded (50 pts, its own rubric). Passing the gate is not worth points; the *quality of the pitch* is.

---

## What is not graded

**Rendering, at any stage.** The question is whether it communicates and whether you decided it on purpose.

**Length beyond the brief.** A longer sequence is not a better one, and it is much harder to hold continuity across.

**Nerves, at the pitch.** See the Pitch rubric.
