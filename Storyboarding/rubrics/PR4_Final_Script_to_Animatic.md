# PR4 Final, Script to Animatic: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 30% of the course grade · Band PR**
**Brief:** [`assignments/PR4_Final_Script_to_Animatic.md`](../assignments/PR4_Final_Script_to_Animatic.md)
**Assessed against CO1 through CO5.**

> Everything, end to end. A script becomes a marked-up script, becomes a beat sheet, becomes a shot list and floor plans, becomes thumbnails, becomes boards, becomes a timed animatic with sound.
>
> The opening question on the brief is the one that matters: **what experience do you want the audience to have?** Answer it before you draw anything and every decision downstream gets easier.

**Milestone chain, seven stages. Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at half its available percentage**, and the cap cascades. Across seven stages that is severe by design: skipping thumbnails and roughs costs 27% outright and caps the 22% of final boards at 11%, which is a 38-point hit on a 100-point rubric. Skipping process here is not a shortcut. It is a failing grade with extra steps.

---

## 1 . Script markup: 7%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 7 | The script is highlighted by shot size, one colour per size, with margin annotations for angle, framing and camera move. Terminology correct. |
| Good | 6 | Fully marked, annotations thin in places. |
| Average | 5 | Highlighted, annotations mostly absent. |
| Developing | 4 | Partial markup, or colour applied with no consistent scheme. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**The cheapest diagnostic in the whole chain, for you and for me.** A long unbroken run of one colour is coverage that never varies. A colour change on every line is cutting for its own sake. Both are visible at a glance, on a page, before a single panel exists.

## 2 . Beat sheet: 6%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 6 | Every beat in order, one line each, with an intended panel count. Structure named: where it turns, what the hook is, what the sequence is about. |
| Good | 5 | Complete, structure named loosely. |
| Average | 4 | Beats listed, no structure identified. |
| Developing | 3 | Prose summary rather than a beat list. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 3 . Shot list and floor plans: 10%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 10 | Framing, angle and script reference for every intended shot, terminology correct. An overhead diagram for every location, camera positions numbered, line of action marked. |
| Good | 9 | Both present, one location undiagrammed. |
| Average | 7 | Shot list complete, floor plans cursory. |
| Developing | 5 | Shot list only. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**The floor plan is the most underused tool in this discipline.** Nobody makes you do it. Everybody who does it has fewer problems. Here, someone is making you do it, and it catches crossed lines before a panel is drawn.

## 4 . Thumbnails: 12%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 12 | The in-class sprint sheet, every beat present, posted the same class. Five designated beats staged three ways each. Choice marked per beat with one line on what the rejected version did better. Delivered on the EX2 working sheet. |
| Good | 10 | Sprint sheet complete, three or four beats explored three ways. |
| Average | 8 | Sprint sheet complete, alternatives thin or unmarked. |
| Developing | 6 | Partial coverage, or panels at final-board size. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

**A variant counts only if it changes shot size, camera angle, blocking, or which character the frame is on.**

The first pass happens in class, timed, roughly ninety seconds a beat, and it is that class's exit ticket. You cannot render anything in ninety seconds, which is exactly why it's ninety seconds. This stage grades exploration, not drawings, and every line on it is countable.

> **A final panel that is a scan, trace or upscale of a thumbnail scores as a missing panel** on the final boards.

## 5 . Rough boards: 15%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 15 | Action and dialogue blocked, legible in every panel. Line held, screen direction consistent, eyelines matched, action matched across cuts. Poses read filled solid black. |
| Good | 13 | Strong throughout, one continuity break or a few ambiguous silhouettes. |
| Average | 11 | Blocking readable, continuity drifts in places. |
| Developing | 8 | Panels exist, staging and continuity largely uncontrolled. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 6 . Final boards, continuity and composition: 13%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 13 | Continuity holds from first panel to last, deliberate breaks identifiable as deliberate. Figure and ground separate, one dominant element, correct headroom/lookroom/leadroom, contrast across the cut, three planes where depth matters. |
| Good | 11 | Strong, with a few panels where two elements compete. |
| Average | 9 | One unintended crossing, or figure and ground merging in several panels. |
| Developing | 7 | Multiple crossings, mid-value figures on mid-value grounds. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 7 . Final boards, staging and acting: 5%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 5 | Every character wants something in every panel. Line of action present. Weight believable. The listener is staged where the reaction is the shot. |
| Good | 4 | Present throughout, a few decorative panels. |
| Average | 3 | Intent visible in the key beats only. |
| Developing | 2 | Characters are placed rather than performing. |
| Missing | 0 | Not assessable. |

## 8 . Final boards, notation and completeness: 5%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 5 | H1 conventions throughout, shot sizes and angles named correctly, correct ratio, panel and shot numbers matched to the script's scene numbers, action described beneath each panel. |
| Good | 4 | Complete, terminology loose in places. |
| Average | 3 | Notation present, numbering not matched to the script. |
| Developing | 2 | Panels with minimal supporting information. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

## 9 . Animatic, timing and sound: 15%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 15 | Pacing reads across the whole piece, rhythm changes where the story changes. Temp music or ambience throughout, scratch dialogue where there's dialogue, sources credited and legally licensed. |
| Good | 13 | Timing reads, one section sits wrong, or sources uncredited. |
| Average | 11 | Workable pacing with passages that drag or rush. Sound present but thin. |
| Developing | 8 | Uniform pacing, or silent. |
| Missing | 0 | No animatic. |

## 10 . Animatic, execution and delivery: 7%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 7 | Pans and pushes supported by artwork drawn wide or large enough. Correct ratio and frame rate, plays cleanly, at least three timing passes evidenced. |
| Good | 6 | Plays cleanly, iteration evidence thin. |
| Average | 5 | Moves reach the edge of their artwork, or only two passes. |
| Developing | 3 | Wrong ratio or rate, or needs coaxing to play. |
| Missing | 0 | Does not play. |

## 11 . Critique participation: 5%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 5 | Substantive, specific notes to at least three other students across the final critiques. Notes received are documented, with one line on what changed. |
| Good | 4 | Three sets of notes given, documentation thin. |
| Average | 3 | Two students. |
| Developing | 2 | One, or present and largely silent. |
| Missing | 0 | Absent. |

Disagreeing with a note is allowed. Ignoring it is not.

## The pitch gate

Every student receives an assigned script. **Nobody is stranded.**

A student may instead pitch their own premise to replace it. The pitch happens before the final is finished, which means it also works as a milestone that catches problems while there's still time.

**The gate is real. A pitch can be declined.** A declined pitch costs nothing but the swap, and the student proceeds on the assigned script with no penalty. A gate that always opens teaches nothing.

The Pitch is separately graded as PR5. Passing the gate is not worth marks; the *quality of the pitch* is.

---

## What is not graded

**Rendering, at any stage.** The question is whether it communicates and whether you decided it on purpose.

**Length beyond the brief.** A longer sequence is not a better one, and it is much harder to hold continuity across. Restraint reads as judgment here, not laziness.

**Nerves, at the pitch.** See the PR5 rubric.
