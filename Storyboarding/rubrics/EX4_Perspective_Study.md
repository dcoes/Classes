# EX4 Perspective Study: Rubric
**Totals 100% of this assignment · 4% of the course grade · Band EX**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX4_Perspective_Study.md`](../assignments/EX4_Perspective_Study.md)
**Assessed against CO1** (*storyboards as a planning tool*) **and CO4** (*visually aesthetic images*).

> One environment, four panels: one-point, two-point, three-point, and the five free depth cues with no construction at all.

---

## 1 . One, two and three point, correct and distinguishable: 30%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 30 | Vanishing points consistent in all three. A stranger can tell the three panels apart. |
| Good | 26 | All three built, one carries a construction error that doesn't break the read. |
| Average | 21 | Two are clean, the third is confused or reverts to a simpler construction. |
| Developing | 15 | One clean construction. The others are eyeballed. |
| Missing | 0 | Not submitted. |

The photo-first track scores identically here: photograph three real locations, trace horizon and vanishing points onto the photos, then draw one of the three. A student who can find the vanishing points in a photograph understands perspective, and the drawing is a separate skill this course is not primarily grading.

## 2 . Camera height declared, and the three differ: 18%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 18 | Each of panels 1 to 3 states where the camera is, the horizon in the drawing matches the stated height, and the three heights are genuinely different. |
| Good | 15 | Declared and matching, but two heights are near-identical. |
| Average | 13 | Declared, and one horizon contradicts its stated height. |
| Developing | 9 | Three identical heights, or declarations with no matching horizon. |
| Missing | 0 | No heights declared. |

**This is the line that carries the concept.** The only idea a student must not leave without is that *the horizon line is the camera height.* Stating it without applying it is half credit, because the student has repeated the concept and not used it.

It also closes a loop backwards. The most common EX3 failure is a low angle drawn with an unmoved horizon. A student who lost points there and gains them here has fixed the underlying misunderstanding, and it's worth saying so on the returned work.

## 3 . The free-cue panel reads as deep: 18%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 18 | Panel 4 achieves real depth using overlap, relative size, atmospheric perspective, value and texture gradient, with **no constructed vanishing points.** |
| Good | 15 | Reads as deep, using three or four of the five cues. |
| Average | 13 | Some depth, mostly from overlap alone. |
| Developing | 9 | Flat, or the cues are present but not doing any work. |
| Missing | 0 | Omitted, **or built with vanishing points.** |

**A panel 4 with vanishing points in it scores zero on this line**, however good it looks. The constraint is the content.

This panel is worth as much as any constructed one, and it should not be graded apologetically. In production it's the panel that actually gets drawn: eighty panels in two days does not include three-point construction on each one. It's overlap, value and relative size, producing in ninety seconds what a constructed drawing needs twenty minutes to reach.

It also carries the fairness of the assignment. A weak draftsman can take full marks here and on the comparison line, which is 26% requiring no construction skill, and that is what stops perspective from being the draftsmanship gate this course claims not to have.

## 4 . Construction visible: 14%

| Band | % | |
|---|--:|---|
| Exemplary | 14 | Lines left on a separate layer, or in a lighter weight, on all three constructed panels. |
| Good | 12 | Visible on two of three. |
| Average | 10 | Visible on one. |
| Developing | 7 | Faint traces only. |
| Missing | 0 | Erased throughout. |

Same principle as showing the arithmetic on EX2: the construction is the graded artifact, and it is the only thing distinguishing a built drawing from a lucky one.

---

## Common failures

Two-point drawn with one vanishing point off-page and unaccounted for. Three-point with the vertical vanishing point ignored. Camera heights declared but identical. Construction lines erased. Panel 4 built with vanishing points.

---

## What is not graded

**Rendering.** A ruled, unshaded, visibly-constructed drawing takes full marks.

**Fidelity to a real place.** Nobody is checking the street corner against the street corner.

---

## Where this goes

Single-stage, no milestone cap. Due at the studio class, so that students who are stuck can be helped at the desk rather than losing the marks silently. Perspective is the topic most likely to make a non-drawer disengage, and the placement is deliberate.
