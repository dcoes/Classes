# EX4 Perspective Study: Rubric
**30 points · Band EX · Assessed against CO1, CO4**
**Brief:** [`assignments/EX4_Perspective_Study.md`](../assignments/EX4_Perspective_Study.md)

> CO1: *Draw storyboards as a planning tool.* CO4: *Highlight visually aesthetic images.*

One environment, four panels: one-point, two-point, three-point, and the five free depth cues with no construction at all.

---

| Component | Pts | CO | Full credit |
|---|--:|---|---|
| **One-, two-, three-point correct and distinguishable** | 9 | CO1 | 3 pts each. Vanishing points consistent; a stranger can tell the three apart. |
| **Camera height declared, and the three differ** | 5 | CO1 | Each of panels 1–3 states where the camera is, and the horizon in the drawing matches the stated height. Three different heights. |
| **Free-cue panel reads as deep** | 5 | CO4 | Panel 4 achieves depth using overlap, size, atmospheric, value and texture: **with no constructed vanishing points.** |
| **Construction visible** | 4 | CO1 | Lines left on a separate layer or in a lighter weight on panels 1–3. |
| **Same environment across all four** | 4 | CO1 | Recognizably one place, four times. |
| **The comparison line** | 3 | CO4 | Time taken and depth gained, per panel, in a sentence or two. |

**Total: 30**

---

## The line that carries the concept

**Camera height, 5 points.** This is the assessment of the only idea in the session that a student must not leave without: *the horizon line is the camera height.*

Full credit requires the declaration **and** the match. A panel labeled "kneeling, 3 feet" with a horizon at two-thirds frame height has stated the concept and not applied it: score half.

Three identical heights across the three panels scores 2 of 5 even if each construction is technically correct, because the student has drawn three perspectives of the same viewpoint and missed that the viewpoint was the variable.

**This connects backwards, deliberately.** The most common EX3 failure is a low angle drawn with an unmoved horizon. A student who lost points there and gains them here has closed the loop, and it's worth saying so on the returned work.

---

## Panel 4 is worth as much as the constructed panels

That is not generosity and it should not be graded apologetically.

In production this is the panel that actually gets drawn. Eighty panels in two days does not include three-point construction on each one. It's overlap, value and relative size, producing depth in ninety seconds that a constructed drawing needs twenty minutes to reach. Construction is for establishing shots, complicated interiors, and spaces returned to repeatedly.

Panel 4 also carries the fairness of the whole assignment. A weak draftsman can score 5/5 on it and 3/3 on the comparison, 8 points that require no construction skill at all, which stops perspective from being the draftsmanship gate this course claims not to have.

**A panel 4 with vanishing points in it scores zero on that line**, however good it looks. The constraint is the content.

---

## The alternative track

Photographing three locations, tracing the horizon and vanishing points onto the photos, then drawing one of the three, scores **identically** on the nine-point line. Identification before construction: a student who can find the vanishing points in a photograph understands perspective, and the drawing is a separate skill this course is not primarily grading.

Panel 4 is required on both tracks.

---

## Common failures

| Failure | Cost |
|---|--:|
| Three panels, three different environments | Up to 4, and the comparison becomes meaningless |
| Two-point drawn with one vanishing point off-page and unaccounted for | Up to 3 |
| Three-point with the vertical vanishing point ignored below the horizon | Up to 3 |
| Camera heights declared but identical | 3 of 5 |
| Horizon contradicts the declared height | Half of 5 |
| Construction lines erased | Up to 4 |
| Panel 4 built with vanishing points | 5 |
| Comparison line omitted | 3 |

---

## What is not graded

**Rendering.** A ruled, unshaded, visibly-constructed drawing is a full-marks drawing.

**Fidelity to a real place.** Nobody is checking the street corner against the street corner.

---

## Milestone note

Single-stage, no cap. Due Session 7, the studio session, so that students who are stuck can be helped at the desk rather than losing the points silently. Perspective is the topic most likely to make a non-drawer disengage, and the placement is deliberate.
