## What the chain looks like

Almost everything downstream depends on **EX2**. The templates built in Class 2 are the delivery format for EX3, EX4, EX5, PR1 and PR4, and the two-sheet structure (a small working sheet and a larger presentation sheet) is what keeps thumbnails and finished boards as physically different objects.

```
EX2 Templates
  |- EX3 Twelve Shots ---------> PR2 Midterm (same skill, at length)
  |- EX4 Perspective
  |- EX5 Continuity Strips
  |- PR1 City Park ------------> PR3 Animatic (same boards, timed)
  '- PR4 Final ----------------> PR5 Pitch (gate + milestone)

EX7 AE Rig -------------------> PR3, PR4 animatics
EX6 Lighting -----------------> PR4 key frames
```

Three pairs are deliberately sequenced so the small one rehearses the large one:

- **EX3 to PR2.** Naming a shot and saying what it does, first on twelve panels of your own, then at length on someone else's sequence.
- **PR1 to PR3.** The same boards, first as a sequence, then as a piece of timed film.
- **EX7 to PR3.** The software, first on a supplied rig, then on your own work.

## What every brief says somewhere

**Clarity and judgment, not rendering.** The question is never "is this well drawn." It is "does this communicate, and did you decide it on purpose."

**What is not graded** is a section in every brief, and it's there because students spend effort on the wrong things by default. Saying what doesn't count is more useful than repeating what does.

**Medium is open where the objective allows it.** EX6 accepts drawing, photography or 3D at equal credit, and EX4 offers a photo-first track. Those assignments assess identifying and controlling something, light or depth, and drawing ability is not that skill.
