Everything this course teaches, end to end, on one sequence.

## The pitch gate

In **Session 14**, before your final is finished, you pitch. This is a separate 50-point assignment with its own rubric — see [`The Pitch`](../rubrics/The_Pitch.md).

If you want to replace the assigned script with your own premise, this is where you ask.

**The gate is real. A pitch can be declined.**

A declined pitch costs you nothing but the swap — you proceed on the assigned script with no penalty of any kind. A gate that always opens teaches nothing, so this one doesn't.

Two things worth knowing:

1. **Passing the gate is not worth points.** The *quality of the pitch* is. A student who pitches brilliantly and gets declined scores higher than one who pitches badly and gets approved.
2. **The pitch is also a milestone.** It happens before the final is done specifically so problems surface while there is still time to fix them. Use it that way.

## Milestone chain

| Stage | Pts |
|---|--:|
| **1 · Beat sheet** | 20 |
| **2 · Shot list + floor plans** | 20 |
| **3 · Thumbnails** | 25 |
| **4 · Rough boards** | 35 |
| **5 · Final boards** | 45 |
| **6 · Animatic** | 45 |
| **7 · Critique participation** | 10 |

> **Each milestone gates the next. A stage submitted without the preceding stage is capped at 50% of that stage's available points.**

**Read this arithmetic before you decide to skip anything.** Across seven stages the cascade is severe on purpose. A student who skips thumbnails and roughs to arrive with finished boards loses 60 points outright and has their 45-point final boards capped at 22.5 — an **82.5-point penalty on a 200-point assignment.**

Skipping process here is not a shortcut. It is a failing grade with extra steps.

---

## What each stage is

**1 · Beat sheet.** Every event in order, one line each, with an intended panel count. Plus: name the structure. Where does it turn? What's the hook? What is this sequence *about*?

**2 · Shot list + floor plans.** Framing, angle, and script reference for every intended shot, using correct terminology (H2). Then an **overhead diagram for every location**, with camera positions numbered and the line of action marked.

> The floor plan is the most underused tool in this discipline. Nobody makes you do it. Everybody who does it has fewer problems. Here, someone is making you do it.

**3 · Thumbnails.** The whole sequence, not the fun parts. At least three beats staged two ways with your choice indicated and briefly justified. Fast and loose — rendered thumbnails are marked down.

**4 · Rough boards.** Action and dialogue blocked. Continuity established: line held, screen direction consistent, eyelines matched. Poses read in silhouette (H4, H6).

**5 · Final boards.** On your A1 template, notated per H1, with H3 and H6 actually run. Every character wants something in every panel. Panels numbered to the script's scene numbers.

**6 · Animatic.** Timed, with sound. Full spec in H5 and in the Animatic brief.

**7 · Critique participation.** Substantive notes to at least three peers across the final critiques. Plus documentation of the notes *you* received and one line on what you changed.

## The standards, restated

You have heard all of these before. They are what the 200 points are for.

- **Clarity over beauty.** The question is never "is this well drawn." It's "does this communicate, and did you decide it on purpose."
- **Silhouette readability.** Fill it black. Can a stranger name the action?
- **Composition is about two frames, not one.** Every panel is a question about how it relates to the one before it.
- **Every character wants something in every panel.** If you can't say what, the panel is decoration.
- **Timing is a decision.** Use the stopwatch.

---

## What is not graded

**Rendering, at any stage.**

**Length beyond the brief.** A longer sequence is not a better one, and it's much harder to hold continuity across. Restraint reads as judgment here, not as laziness.

**Nerves at the pitch.** Defending your work instead of hearing the note does.

---

## One honest note about the field

The part of this job that is *"make a picture of a thing"* is under real pressure. I'm not going to tell you otherwise.

The part that is *"decide what the picture should be, and know why"* is the part this assignment is built around, and it's the part that's still yours. That isn't reassurance. It's the reason this course grades clarity and judgment instead of rendering, and it's the reason the pitch is on the syllabus at all.
