# EX1 — Diagnostic Boards
**10 points · Pass / revise · Assigned Session 1 · Due Session 2**
**Rubric:** [`rubrics/EX1_Diagnostic_Boards.md`](../rubrics/EX1_Diagnostic_Boards.md)
**Handout:** H1 (Notation)

---

## Read this first

**This is not a test, and you cannot fail it by drawing badly.**

It is pass / revise. If it comes back marked *revise*, you redraw it and it passes. Nobody in the history of this course has lost these ten points for the quality of a drawing, and nobody will.

What it's actually for: I need to see how everyone draws before I start teaching, and **you need to draw something before you have time to get nervous about it.** Both of those work better on day one than on day six.

---

## Part 1A — Four panels

Board a sequence you have personally performed a few hundred times: **going to class.**

| Panel | Shot |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Establishing shot** of the building from outside. |
| **2** | The student in the hallway, among other people. |
| **3** | The student entering the classroom door. |
| **4** | The lecture — **from the student's point of view**, seated. |

Four panels. That's the whole assignment.

**Panel 4 is the one that matters.** It's a POV shot, which means the character whose story this is doesn't appear in it — you're looking through their eyes. Three panels of watching someone, then one panel of *being* them. You have seen this thousands of times without noticing it, and this is the first time you have to build one.

Don't over-think it. Do notice that panels 1 through 3 get progressively closer and more interior, and that this happens without anyone deciding it should. That's a pattern, and this course is largely about learning to see them.

## Part 1B — One panel

A short prose passage will be posted. **Illustrate it in a single panel.**

You are not illustrating the whole thing. You are picking **the one moment** that carries it, and drawing that. Which moment you choose is the interesting part, and we will talk about the range of answers in Session 2 — there will be a range, and that's the point.

*(You do not need the textbook. The passage is posted.)*

---

## What to deliver

Five panels total. **Paper and pencil is completely fine** — photograph it with your phone and post the photo. Digital is fine too. No template required; you don't have one yet.

Underneath each panel, one line of action description, present tense. That's the only notation asked for at this stage.

---

## What is not graded

**Drawing quality. At all. In any respect.**

Stick figures pass. Stick figures with clearly staged action pass comfortably. This is graded on whether the five panels exist and whether each one is a legible attempt at the shot described.

---

## Why this is day one

Two reasons, and both are worth saying plainly.

**I need a baseline.** In a mixed room — animation, film, game production, and a reliable number of people who have not drawn since childhood — I have no idea what I'm looking at until I've seen everyone draw once. That changes how the next fifteen sessions get taught. It doesn't change anyone's grade.

**Waiting makes it worse.** The longer you go without drawing in a drawing-adjacent course, the more the first attempt costs you. So the first attempt happens now, for ten points, on a subject you already know by heart, with the explicit promise that badly is fine.

**If you have never drawn before, you can do well in this course.** If you draw beautifully and won't make decisions, you will not. That's the actual standard, and it starts here.
