# EX4 Perspective Study
**4% of the course grade · Assigned Class 6 · Due Class 7**
**Rubric:** [`rubrics/EX4_Perspective_Study.md`](../rubrics/EX4_Perspective_Study.md)

---

## The assignment

**One environment. Four panels. Four different ways of building depth.**

Pick a space with some structure in it: a street corner, a classroom, a stairwell, a warehouse, a subway platform. Something with straight edges and more than one plane. Then draw it four times:

| Panel | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **One-point perspective** |
| **2** | **Two-point perspective** |
| **3** | **Three-point perspective** |
| **4** | **No constructed perspective at all**: the five free depth cues only |

Same environment in all four. On your **1.85: 1 template.**

---

## Panels 1–3 · Declare the camera height

Each of the first three panels carries a written note: **where the camera is.**

> *"Eye level, standing: 5'6"."*
> *"Kneeling, about 3 feet."*
> *"Second-floor window, roughly 15 feet up, looking down."*

**And the three must be different heights.**

Here is why this line is on the assignment. The horizon line in your drawing **is** the camera height. Not a related idea, not a metaphor: the same thing. Put the horizon low and you are kneeling. Put it high and you are on a balcony. Put it off the top of the panel and you are on the roof.

Which means perspective and camera angle are not two subjects. **They are one subject with two names**, and you have already met the other name: in Class 3, where you drew a low angle and a high angle and where the most common failure was drawing a tilted head instead of a moved camera. That failure and this assignment are the same problem.

If you lost points on EX3 for an unmoved horizon, this is the fix.

**Leave your construction lines visible.** Separate layer, or a lighter weight. Same principle as showing your arithmetic on EX2: the construction is the graded artifact, and it's the only thing distinguishing a built drawing from an eyeballed one.

---

## Panel 4 · The five free cues

Draw the same environment again, deep and convincing, using **none of the machinery**. No vanishing points, no horizon construction, no rulers.

Only these:

| Cue | |
|---|---|
| **Overlap** | Put something in front of something. The cheapest depth there is. |
| **Relative size** | Same object, smaller, reads as further. |
| **Atmospheric perspective** | Distance washes out: lighter, lower contrast, less saturated. |
| **Value** | Near is darker and higher contrast. Far is lighter and flatter. |
| **Texture gradient** | Detail compresses with distance. Bricks become a texture, then a tone. |

**This panel is worth as many points as any of the constructed ones.**

Not as a consolation. Because in production, this is the panel you actually draw. A board artist producing eighty panels in two days is not setting up three-point construction on each of them. They're using overlap, value and size, and getting depth in ninety seconds that a constructed drawing takes twenty minutes to reach.

Construction is for when you need it: an establishing shot, a complicated interior, a space you'll return to twenty times. The rest of the time, the free cues carry it.

---

## The comparison line

One or two sentences at the bottom of the page:

> **How long did each panel take, and how much depth did each one buy?**

Time them. Actually time them. This is the whole argument of the assignment reduced to a number, and you will find the ratio surprising.

## If you would rather not draw first

An alternative route through panels 1–3, worth the same:

1. **Photograph three real locations**: one that reads as one-point, one as two-point, one as three-point.
2. **Trace the construction onto the photographs.** Find the horizon. Find the vanishing points. Extend the lines.
3. **Then draw one of the three.**

Identification before construction. If you can find the vanishing points in a photograph, you understand perspective; the drawing is a separate skill and this course is not primarily grading it. Panel 4 is required either way.

## Format

- **1.85: 1 panels on your EX2 template**, four panels
- Camera height written under panels 1–3
- Construction lines visible on 1–3
- Comparison line at the bottom

---

## What is not graded

**Rendering.** A ruled, unshaded, obviously-constructed drawing is a full-marks drawing here.

**Accuracy of the real place.** Nobody is checking your street corner against the street corner.

---

## Why this assignment exists

Perspective is the part of this course most likely to make somebody quit, because it looks like a draftsmanship gate and draftsmanship gates are exactly what this course claims not to be.

So it's built to not be one. Panel 4 is available to anyone. The photo track is available to anyone. And the actual content, **the horizon is the camera**, is a conceptual point, not a manual skill. You can hold it completely without being able to draw a convincing cube.

Depth is a system with several independent inputs. Construction is one of them. It is not the important one.
