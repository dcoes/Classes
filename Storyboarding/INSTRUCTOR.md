## Class 1: Intro to Storyboarding
**Deck 01 · Handout H1 · Exit ticket: ten poses**
**Assigned:** EX1 Diagnostic

**Timing.** ~50 min lecture · 20 min the real-boards look · break · 40 min silhouette work and ten poses · 15 min EX1 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **Say the diagnostic is a diagnostic.** It calibrates the rest of the term and changes nobody's grade. A room told this submits honest work. A room not told submits careful work, which is useless for the purpose.
- **Panel 4 is the only one with a wrong answer.** POV means the character isn't in frame. Expect a third of the room to draw the student sitting at a desk. Catch it in week one, because the same error resurfaces in EX3, and a student who misses it twice needs a conversation rather than a second deduction.
- **1B has no wrong answer, and the spread is the point.** Collect the range and show it in class 2. Demonstrating that *which moment you draw is a decision* lands far harder as fifteen different answers on a screen than as a claim in a lecture.
- Forward: silhouette readability introduced today recurs in composition, staging, lighting, games and every critique. Name it as a standard that will keep coming back, so the callbacks read as a pattern rather than repetition.

**Have open:** the professional production boards, the 1B passage posted.

## Class 2: Origins and Aspect Ratios
**Deck 02 · Exit ticket: ratio arithmetic, three numbers**
**Due:** EX1 · **Assigned:** EX2 Templates, and **PR2 the midterm is briefed today**

**Timing.** ~15 min the 1B spread from class 1 · 45 min ratio history · break · 30 min worked arithmetic live · 40 min Photoshop demo and build time · 10 min midterm brief

**Note for instructor**
- **Brief the midterm today.** It needs the lead time: the assignment requires watching a sequence three times and it cannot be done in a week. This is a change from how it ran before, where it arrived late.
- **Do the arithmetic on a board, slowly, with a calculator visible.** The worked table in the deck is the backup, not the delivery.
- **The 8 x 4 grid is the point of EX2 and it isn't obvious.** Everything fits on paper. What students discover by measuring is that the panels come out two thirds of an inch wide, which is a thumbnail sheet. Let them find it. The presentation sheet they then derive is what makes thumbnails and finals physically different objects, which is what stops thumbnail reuse on the final project.
- **2.35 versus 2.39.** The deck carries both. 2.35 was CinemaScope until SMPTE revised the aperture in 1970; 2.39 has been standard since. The assignment builds 2.39. The A-grade version of this conversation is that "widescreen" was a commercial argument between studios and television, not a fixed standard.
- **The Photoshop demo is not optional and not a formality.** The prerequisite isn't reliably enforced. Ten minutes on file formats and guides prevents losing an assignment to something that isn't the subject.
- Fixed previously and worth not re-breaking: the McCay bet is conventionally attributed to **George McManus**, not Ed McMahon. CinemaScope, not "Cinescope."

**Have open:** Photoshop or Photopea, a calculator, the class 1 1B collection, the midterm sequence.

## Class 3: Fundamentals of the Shot
**Deck 03 · Handout H2 · Exit ticket: name every shot in a 60-second clip**
**Due:** EX2 · **Assigned:** EX3 Twelve Shots

**Timing.** ~70 min shot taxonomy · break · 30 min clip breakdown live · 30 min first three panels of EX3 in class

**Note for instructor**
- **State the EX3 same-subject constraint twice, and once more in writing.** Left alone, students produce twelve unrelated drawings, which teaches nothing because everything changes at once. One subject, one location, twelve cameras.
- **Warn about OTS and POV before they choose a location.** Both need a second person or a looked-at object. The common failure is reaching panel nine and having to restart.
- **Do the first three panels in class.** The never-drawn students need to watch it happen at speed before attempting it alone.
- H2 is issued today and is open-book for the midterm. Say that now, so it survives to class 8.
- Forward: the caption requirement, *what the shot is for* rather than what it is, is rehearsal for PR2's written analysis. A student who loses those marks and doesn't correct course loses them again on the 25% assignment.

**Have open:** the 60-second clip, H2 printed.

## Class 4: Continuity, The Rules
**Deck 08a · Handout H4 · Exit ticket: fix two line-breaking panels by moving the camera**
**Due:** EX3 · **Assigned:** PR1 City Park

**Timing.** ~60 min the rules · break · 30 min live floor-plan demo · 30 min exit exercise · 20 min PR1 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **This is placed before PR1 on purpose** (`PROJECT.md` §9). PR1 is graded on these rules, so teaching them afterward would be indefensible. Don't let the deck slip a class.
- **The floor plan introduced today is a recurring tool, not a one-off.** It returns in EX4's camera heights, EX6's light plans, PR4's shot list, and Deck 10's level layouts. Name it as a system the first time. The payoff is students recognising it in class 7 without being told.
- **The exit ticket is the whole lesson compressed:** fixing a crossed line by *moving the camera* rather than redrawing the characters. Students reach for redrawing first, and that instinct is the misconception worth breaking today.
- PR1 is constrained on purpose and students read constraint as a lack of ambition. Say plainly that adding a car chase costs marks, and why: continuity is harder to hold across complexity, and continuity is the entire subject.

**Have open:** H4 printed, whiteboard for floor plans.

## Class 5: Composition
**Deck 05 · Handout H3 · Exit ticket: run H3 on three panels, post one before/after**
**Due:** PR1 beat sheet

**Timing.** ~75 min composition · break · 25 min EX3 submissions on screen · 40 min checklist work on their own panels

**Note for instructor**
- **Put EX3 submissions on the screen.** Twelve shots of one subject, from twenty students, is the best available demonstration that the camera is a variable, and it costs nothing to assemble because they just handed it in.
- **The magician frame and ballistics** are the two ideas from this deck that recur. Ballistics, information having momentum across cuts, is the one students remember and the one that changes their PR1 roughs.
- Figure and ground is the number one panel failure and has nothing to do with drawing ability. The squint test is checkable, teachable, and fixes half a student's panels in ten minutes. Weight it accordingly.
- Forward: H3's three-value section returns as the graded artifact in EX6. Plant it now.

**Have open:** H3 printed, EX3 submissions loaded.

## Class 6: Perspective
**Deck 06 · Exit ticket: name the depth cues in three stills**
**Due:** PR1 thumbnails · **Assigned:** EX4 Perspective

**Timing.** ~30 min the five free depth cues · 45 min construction · break · 30 min live demo · 35 min EX4 start

**Note for instructor**
- **Lead with the free cues, not with construction.** The deck was rebuilt in that order deliberately. It is the one deck that used to contradict the course's own grading philosophy by making draftsmanship the gate.
- **"The horizon line is your camera height" is the sentence of the class.** Connect it directly to the EX3 low/high angle failures you just graded: a tilted head is not a moved camera, and the horizon is the tell. Students who lost marks there get the fix today, and that connection is worth making out loud by name.
- **EX4 panel 4 is the fairness valve:** free depth cues only, no vanishing points, full marks available to a weak draftsman. Say it's worth as much as the constructed panels and mean it. Don't present it apologetically.
- The photo-first alternative track is equal credit. Offer it in class rather than waiting for someone to ask, which is the same principle EX6 runs on.

**Have open:** three stills for the exit ticket, ruler for the live demo.

## Class 7: Lighting
**Deck 07 · Handout H7 · Exit ticket: light one panel three ways**
**Due:** PR1 roughs · EX4 · **Assigned:** EX6 Lighting

**Timing.** ~35 min identification first · 35 min the six setups · break · 25 min three-value reduction demo · 30 min EX6 start · 15 min PR1 desk checks

**Note for instructor**
- **Say the medium list out loud before anyone raises a hand.** Drawing, phone photo with a desk lamp, 3D render, value study, all equal credit. This is the sentence that ends years of ad-hoc accommodations, and it lands very differently offered than granted.
- **Identification comes first, structurally.** Part 1 is 30% a non-drawer earns on the actual objective before any making happens. That ordering is the assignment's argument and the lecture should mirror it.
- **Demo the three-value reduction live on three media.** Posterize a photo, a render and a drawing side by side and let the room watch them become the same class of object. That demo *is* the fairness argument, and it's more convincing than stating it.
- **High key versus low contrast is the failure that costs most.** Two dials, independent: level and contrast. If a student's high key and low contrast look alike, they're working one dial. Put the 2x2 on the board.
- Forward and backward at once: the light plan is class 4's floor plan, the three-value reduction is H3's discipline, the naming test is class 1's silhouette test. **Name all three connections explicitly.** This is the class where the course's systems argument is most visible.
- Deck 07 was rebuilt from a state where speaker notes desynchronized from slide 25. If notes stop matching slides, that's a regression, not a mystery.

**Have open:** H7 printed, the eight EX6 stills, three images ready to posterize live.

## Class 8: Midterm critique
**No deck · Long class, no lecture · Exit ticket: two written notes to two peers**
**Due:** PR1 final boards · **PR2 Midterm** · **Assigned:** *nothing*

**Timing.** Full class of critique. Budget ~6 min per student and hold to it.

**Note for instructor**
- **This is a heavy due date.** PR1 finals and PR2 land together, which is the cost of keeping both. Flag it in class 6 and again in class 7 so it isn't a surprise.
- **Nothing is assigned today.** Deliberate, after a long assignment, for both sides. Resist filling it.
- **Establish the critique rules out loud, once, before the first piece:** the note is data, not a verdict; the artist does not respond except to ask a clarifying question; they may respond after all notes are in. These are the same rules PR5 is graded on in five classes, so consistency now pays later.
- **Giving notes is CO5 evidence and is recorded.** Say so. A student who submits work and gives no notes has met the other objectives and not that one.
- Keep a running list of the recurring failures across the room. It's the best possible input for how hard to push in class 9.

## Class 10: Continuity, The Cut
**Deck 08b · Exit ticket: change one transition, report what changed**
**Due:** PR4 M1 script markup and beat sheet · **Assigned:** EX5 Continuity Strips

**Timing.** ~60 min the cut · break · 30 min sequence breakdown · 40 min EX5 start

**Note for instructor**
- **The cut-away example was rebuilt and it's worth knowing why.** The old version, a bus speeding and people at the stop watching it pass, never left the main action's space or time, which makes it a **reaction shot**. That's why students could never distinguish it from the cut-in and why the strip felt weak. The new version cuts genuinely elsewhere and requires the student to name the job: buys time, or gives the audience what the character doesn't have.
- **Cut-in, cut-away and reaction shot are three tools**, and students routinely submit the same thing three times. Draw all three on the board against one scenario.
- **Strip 3's BROKEN panel is graded as correct work**, not penalised. Producing a violation on purpose is the only evidence available that a student can see the rule rather than recite it. Say this when assigning, or half the room will think it's a trick.
- **Cross-cutting, strip 6, is the highest-value item here** and the only device that needs the strip format. If time is short, cut strip 3's fourth panel, not strip 6.
- EX5 is spaced retrieval, not a re-teach. The rules came in class 4, six classes ago, which is the point.

## Class 11: Animatics and After Effects
**Decks 12 and 13 · Handouts H5, H8 · Exit ticket: time three of your own panels**
**Due:** PR4 M2 shot list and floor plans · **Assigned:** EX7 AE Rig, and **PR3 Animatic**

**Timing.** ~45 min timing and the silent-then-scored demo · break · 60 min AE build-along, all five steps · 25 min PR3 and EX7 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **This class carries two decks and it is tight.** If something has to give, shorten Deck 12's history and keep the build-along whole, because the build-along is the only structured AE exposure many of these students get.
- **The silent-then-scored demo is the best twenty seconds in Deck 12.** Play a clip silent, then with music under it. Nothing makes the case for temp audio faster.
- **Run the AE build-along with everyone's screen up.** Step 2, anchor point to the shoulder, is where the class splits. An arm rotating around its own middle still reads as a wave in the render, so it has to be caught live.
- **Step 5 is the designed obstacle and students will report it as a broken file.** It isn't. Boards and reference art arrive flat, and anything passing behind anything else needs a mask. Let them hit it before explaining it.
- **The closing question is the point:** *what would you have had to plan in the drawing to make this move possible?* Without it this is a tutorial, and `CLAUDE.md` §3 says software is never the subject. Don't skip it for time.
- **Boundary:** CA 140 owns AE as an animatic tool, five operations. Motion Graphics owns it as a craft. If the deck starts teaching easing or effects, cut it back (`CURRICULUM.md`).
- **"A prototype of the film experience, not of the film"** is the clearest reframe available for why timing beats drawing here.

**Have open:** the EX7 `.psd`, H5 and H8 printed, a clip to run silent and scored, a stopwatch.

## Class 12: Studio
**No deck · Exit ticket: the PR4 thumbnail sprint sheet**
**Due:** EX5 · EX7

**Timing.** ~45 min the thumbnail sprint, timed · remainder scheduled desk crits, ~8 min each, posted rotation

**Note for instructor**
- **The exit ticket is PR4's thumbnail milestone.** Ninety seconds a beat, on paper, photographed and posted before anyone leaves. This single mechanism handles three problems at once: you can't render in ninety seconds, the work is demonstrably theirs, and the first pass happens in a room you're standing in.
- **This class used to be open work time and attendance decayed through it.** The change is that crits are *scheduled*, on a posted list with times. A student with a slot at 2:40 has a reason to be in the room at 2:40.
- **This is also the Photoshop and AE catch-up class.** Students who bluffed through class 2 or class 11 surface here. Handle it at the desk without ceremony.
- Two agendas alongside the sprint: PR4 shot lists need a continuity check, and PR3 needs a nudge, because it's due next class and most students haven't started.

**Have open:** the crit rotation, posted in advance. Paper for the sprint.

## Class 13: The Pitch, and final check
**Deck 11 · Exit ticket: notes received and one line on what changes**
**Due:** PR3 Animatic · PR4 M4 rough boards · **pitches run today**

**Timing.** ~45 min deck · break · pitches for the remainder, scheduled

**Note for instructor**

**Running the pitches**
- **Schedule it and post the order.** 3 to 4 min pitch plus 2 min of notes is ~6 min per student. Twenty students will not fit in one class alongside the deck, so either shorten to 2 minutes each, run some pitches into class 14 before the critique, or record the overflow.
- **Notes come from you and from three assigned peers, rotating.** Peers giving notes earn CO5 credit for it, and that's the mechanism that makes the objective assessable for the whole room rather than just the presenter.
- **State the rule out loud before the first pitch:** the artist does not respond during notes except to ask a clarifying question, and may respond after all notes are in. Enforce it on the first pitch or it won't hold.
- **The 24-hour follow-up is what converts the pitch from a performance into a revision mechanism.** Notes received, plus one line on what changes. Without it the notes evaporate.

**On the content**
- **Nerves are not a deduction. Defending instead of hearing the note is.** Say it before the first pitch, not after someone gets defensive.
- **The gate has to be real.** A pitch can be declined, and a declined pitch costs nothing but the swap. Also worth saying: passing the gate isn't worth marks, the *quality of the pitch* is, so a brilliant declined pitch outscores a poor approved one.
- **Boundary with Game Design:** they own the business pitch, market, platform, profitability, client POV. CA 140 owns the sequence pitch. Say the distinction in the room: *a business pitch sells the idea; a story pitch makes the room see the film.*
- The answer to nerves, worth borrowing verbatim from the Game Design deck: *"because as the designer, no one knows more about the project than you do."* True of the board artist too.
- The honest job-market slide sits here deliberately. Deliver it plainly, without reassurance. That's why it works.

**Swap slot.** If the cohort is mostly game production majors, Deck 10 Boarding for Games is the strongest substitution for the lecture half of this class, with pitches moved into class 14. Same for the modules in `modules/`.

**Have open:** the pitch schedule, posted in advance. Wall space or a projector for boards.

## Standing notes

**The three fairness mechanisms are one mechanism.** The silhouette test (class 1, all term), EX5's naming test, and EX6's naming test all ask *does this read* rather than *is this well made*, and all three can be run by the student before submitting. When you name one, it's worth naming the others. The pattern is the lesson, not the individual test.

**The floor plan is the course's recurring tool.** Classes 4, 6, 7, 9, 11 and the games material. Every appearance is a chance to make the systems argument concrete.

**Medium-open assignments are open by design, not by concession.** EX6 and EX4 assess identifying and controlling something, and drawing is a different skill. Offering the options unprompted is the difference between a designed assignment and an accommodation, and students can tell which one they're being handed.

**Exit tickets are graded on existing, not on quality.** Ten to ninety seconds of work, no make-ups. They exist to make the lecture land on a decision, and to give work classes an attendance floor.

**Nothing in this file should migrate into a deck.** If a note here needs to fire at a specific slide, it may go in that slide's speaker notes prefixed exactly `Note for instructor:`. That's the only sanctioned exception (`CLAUDE.md` §4).

**Fourteen classes is tight.** Deck 10 and the four modules in `modules/` have no permanent slot and are built as swap-ins. If a term runs longer, Deck 10 is the first thing to promote.
