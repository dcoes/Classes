## S1 — Intro to Storyboarding
**Deck 01 · Handout H1 · Exit ticket: ten poses**

**Due:** — **Assigned:** EX1 Diagnostic Boards

**Timing.** ~50 min lecture · 20 min the real-boards look · break · 40 min silhouette work + ten poses · 15 min EX1 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **Say the diagnostic is a diagnostic.** It calibrates the next fifteen sessions and changes nobody's grade. A room told this submits honest work; a room not told submits careful work, which is useless for the purpose.
- **Panel 4 is the only one with a wrong answer.** POV means the character isn't in frame. Expect a third of the room to draw the student sitting at a desk. Catch it on day two — the same error resurfaces in EX3, and a student who misses it twice needs a conversation rather than a second deduction.
- **1B has no wrong answer, and the spread is the point.** Collect the range and show it in S2. Demonstrating that *which moment you draw is a decision* lands far harder as fifteen different answers on a screen than as a claim in a lecture.
- Forward: silhouette readability introduced today recurs in composition, staging, lighting, games, and every critique. Name it as a standard that will keep coming back, so the callbacks land as a pattern rather than repetition.

**Have open:** the professional production boards, the 1B passage posted.

## S2 — Aspect Ratios
**Deck 02 · Exit ticket: ratio arithmetic, three numbers**

**Due:** EX1 **Assigned:** EX2 Template Build

**Timing.** ~15 min the 1B spread from S1 · 45 min ratio history · break · 30 min worked arithmetic live · 40 min Photoshop demo + build time

**Note for instructor**
- **Do the arithmetic on a board, slowly, with a calculator visible.** The worked table in the deck is the backup, not the delivery.
- **Do not rescue anyone from the 1.33 overflow.** Eight rows genuinely does not fit on letter portrait and the assignment is built on discovering it. If a student solves it by shrinking the margin below 2″, that's the wrong answer and the rubric says so — the point is that frame shape has consequences, not that grids are flexible.
- **The Photoshop demo is not optional and not a formality.** The prerequisite isn't reliably enforced. Ten minutes on file formats and guides prevents a fifty-point loss for something that isn't the subject.
- **2.35 vs 2.39.** The deck carries both and the assignment builds 2.35. The A-grade version of this conversation is that "widescreen" was a commercial argument between studios and television, not a fixed standard — which is the session's actual content.
- Slides 27–30 were re-opened to match the 2.35 spec. If they read 2.39 anywhere, that's a regression.
- Fixed here previously and worth not re-breaking: the McCay bet is conventionally attributed to **George McManus**, not Ed McMahon. CinemaScope, not "Cinescope."

**Have open:** Photoshop or Photopea, a calculator, the S1 1B collection.

## S3 — Fundamentals of the Shot
**Deck 03 · Handout H2 · Exit ticket: name every shot in a 60-second clip**

**Due:** EX2 **Assigned:** EX3 Twelve Shots · **PR2 Midterm**

**Timing.** ~70 min shot taxonomy · break · 30 min clip breakdown live · 30 min first three panels of EX3 in class · 10 min PR2 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **PR2 is assigned today.** It was never scheduled anywhere in the old paperwork and students used to receive it late. Five sessions of lead time is what makes the "watch it three times" instruction realistic.
- **State the EX3 same-subject constraint twice, and once more in writing.** Left to themselves, students produce twelve unrelated drawings, which teaches nothing because everything changes at once. One subject, one location, twelve cameras.
- **Warn about OTS and POV before they choose a location.** Both need a second person or a looked-at object. The common failure is a student reaching panel nine and having to restart.
- **Do the first three panels in class.** The never-drawn students need to watch it happen at speed before they attempt it alone.
- H2 is issued today and is open-book for the midterm. Say that now, so it survives to Session 8.
- Forward: the caption requirement — *what the shot is for*, not what it is — is rehearsal for PR2's written analysis. A student who loses those six points and doesn't correct course loses them again on a hundred-point assignment.

**Have open:** the 60-second clip, H2 printed.

## S4 — Continuity: The Rules
**Deck 08a · Handout H4 · Exit ticket: fix two line-breaking panels by moving the camera**

**Due:** — **Assigned:** PR1 City Park

**Timing.** ~60 min the rules · break · 30 min live floor-plan demo · 30 min exit exercise · 20 min PR1 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **This is placed before PR1 on purpose** (`PROJECT.md` §9). PR1 is graded on these rules, so teaching them afterward would be indefensible. Don't let the deck slip a session.
- **The floor plan introduced today is a recurring tool, not a one-off.** It returns in EX4's camera heights, PR4's shot list, EX6's light plans, and Deck 10's level layouts. Name it as a system the first time — the payoff is students recognising it in S11 without being told.
- **The exit ticket is the whole lesson compressed:** fixing a crossed line by *moving the camera* rather than redrawing the characters. Students reach for redrawing first. That instinct is the misconception worth breaking today.
- PR1 is constrained on purpose and students read constraint as a lack of ambition. Say plainly that adding a car chase costs points, and why: continuity is harder to hold across complexity, and continuity is the entire subject.

**Have open:** H4 printed, whiteboard for floor plans.

## S6 — Perspective
**Deck 06 · Exit ticket: name the depth cues in three stills**

**Due:** PR1 thumbnails **Assigned:** EX4 Perspective Study

**Timing.** ~30 min the five free depth cues · 45 min construction · break · 30 min live demo · 35 min EX4 start

**Note for instructor**
- **Lead with the free cues, not with construction.** The deck was rebuilt in that order deliberately — it is the one deck that used to contradict the course's own grading philosophy by making draftsmanship the gate.
- **"The horizon line is your camera height" is the sentence of the session.** Connect it directly to the EX3 low/high angle failures you just graded: a tilted head is not a moved camera, and the horizon is the tell. Students who lost points there get the fix today, and that connection is worth making out loud by name.
- **EX4 panel 4 is the fairness valve** — free depth cues only, no vanishing points, full marks available to a weak draftsman. Say it's worth as much as the constructed panels and mean it. Don't present it apologetically.
- The photo-first alternative track is equal credit. Offer it in class rather than waiting for someone to ask, which is the same principle EX6 runs on.
- This is the topic most likely to make a non-drawer disengage. EX4 is due at the studio session for exactly that reason.

**Have open:** three stills for the exit ticket, ruler/straightedge for the live demo.

**No deck · Long session, no lecture · Exit ticket: two written notes to two peers**

**Timing.** Full session of critique. Budget ~6 min per student and hold to it.

## S10 — Continuity: The Cut
**Deck 08b · Exit ticket: change one transition, report what changed**

**Due:** PR4 M1 script markup + beat sheet **Assigned:** EX5 Continuity Strips

**Timing.** ~60 min the cut · break · 30 min sequence breakdown · 40 min EX5 start

**Note for instructor**
- **The cut-away example was rebuilt and it's worth knowing why.** The old version — a bus speeding, people at the stop watching it pass — never left the main action's space or time, which makes it a **reaction shot**. That's why students could never distinguish it from the cut-in, and why the strip felt weak. The new version cuts genuinely elsewhere and requires the student to name the job: buys time, or gives the audience what the character doesn't have.
- **Cut-in, cut-away and reaction shot are three tools** and students routinely submit the same thing three times. Draw all three on the board against one scenario.
- **Strip 3's BROKEN panel is graded as correct work**, not penalized. Producing a violation on purpose is the only evidence available that a student can see the rule rather than recite it. Say this when assigning, or half the room will think it's a trick.
- **Cross-cutting (strip 6) is the highest-value item here** and the only device that requires the strip format to demonstrate. If time is short, cut strip 3's fourth panel, not strip 6.
- EX5 is spaced retrieval, not a re-teach — the rules came in S4, six sessions ago, which is the point.

## S11 — Lighting
**Deck 07 · Handout H7 · Exit ticket: light one panel three ways**

**Due:** PR4 M2 shot list + floor plans **Assigned:** EX6 Lighting Study

**Timing.** ~35 min identification first · 35 min the six setups · break · 25 min three-value reduction demo · 45 min EX6 start

**Note for instructor**
- **Say the medium list out loud before anyone raises a hand.** Drawing, phone photo with a desk lamp, 3D render, value study — all equal credit. This is the sentence that ends years of ad-hoc accommodations, and it lands very differently offered than granted.
- **Identification comes first, structurally.** Part 1 is 12 points a non-drawer earns on the actual objective before any making happens. That ordering is the assignment's argument and the lecture should mirror it.
- **Demo the three-value reduction live on three media** — posterize a photo, a render and a drawing side by side, and let the room see that they become the same class of object. That demo *is* the fairness argument, and it's more convincing than stating it.
- **High key vs. low contrast is the failure that costs most.** Two dials, independent: level and contrast. If a student's #3 and #6 look alike they're working one dial. Put the 2×2 table on the board.
- Forward and backward at once: the light plan is S4's floor plan, the three-value reduction is H3's discipline, the naming test is S1's silhouette test. **Name all three connections explicitly** — this is the session where the course's systems argument is most visible, and it's worth spending two minutes on.
- Deck 07 was rebuilt from a state where speaker notes desynchronized from slide 25 onward. If notes stop matching slides, that's a regression, not a mystery.

## S12 — After Effects for Boards
**Deck 13 · Handout H8 · Exit ticket: the thumbnail sprint sheet**

**Due:** EX5 · PR4 M3 thumbnails **Assigned:** EX7 AE Rig & Move

**Timing.** ~75 min build-along, all five steps · break · 45 min the thumbnail sprint · 15 min EX7 briefing

**Note for instructor**
- **The exit ticket is PR4's thumbnail milestone.** Ninety seconds a beat, on paper, photographed and posted before anyone leaves. This single mechanism handles three problems at once — you can't render in ninety seconds, the work is demonstrably theirs, and the first pass happens in a room you're standing in.
- **Run the build-along with everyone's screen up.** Step 2 — anchor point to the shoulder — is where the class splits. An arm rotating around its own middle still reads as a wave in the render, so it has to be caught live.
- **Step 5 is the designed obstacle and students will report it as a broken file.** It isn't. Boards and reference art arrive flat, and anything passing behind anything else needs a mask. Let them hit it before explaining it.
- **The closing question is the point of the session:** *what would you have had to plan in the drawing to make this move possible?* Without it this is a tutorial, and `CLAUDE.md` §1 says software is never the subject. Don't skip it for time.
- **Boundary:** CA 140 owns AE as an animatic tool — five operations. Motion Graphics owns it as a craft. If the deck starts teaching easing or effects, cut it back (`CURRICULUM.md`).
- Say plainly that PR3 is due three sessions from now and is much harder for anyone who skips this. The failure mode here isn't poor work, it's no work.

## S14 — The Pitch
**Deck 11 · Exit ticket: notes received + one line on what changes**

**Due:** PR4 M4 roughs · EX7 · **pitches begin** **Assigned:** —

**Timing.** ~50 min deck · break · pitches for the rest, scheduled

**Note for instructor**

**Running the pitches**
- **Schedule it and post the order.** 3–4 min pitch plus 2 min of notes is ~6 min per student. Twenty students is two full sessions; twelve is one plus change. That's why pitches span S14 and S15.
- **Notes come from you and from three assigned peers, rotating.** Peers giving notes earn CO5 credit for it — that's the mechanism that makes CO5 assessable for the whole room rather than just the presenter.
- **State the rule out loud before the first pitch:** the artist does not respond during notes except to ask a clarifying question, and may respond after all notes are in. Enforce it on the first pitch or it won't hold for the rest.
- **The 24-hour follow-up is what converts the pitch from a performance into a revision mechanism** — notes received, plus one line on what changes. Without it, the notes evaporate.

**On the content**
- **Nerves are not a deduction. Defending instead of hearing the note is.** Say it before the first pitch, not after someone gets defensive.
- **The gate has to be real.** A pitch can be declined; a declined pitch costs nothing but the swap. A gate that always opens teaches nothing. Also worth saying: passing the gate isn't worth points — the *quality of the pitch* is, so a brilliant declined pitch outscores a poor approved one.
- **Boundary with Game Design:** they own the business pitch — market, platform, profitability, client POV. CA 140 owns the sequence pitch. Say the distinction in the room: *a business pitch sells the idea; a story pitch makes the room see the film.* Students who've taken both should hear that this is a specialization of something they already have.
- The answer to nerves, worth borrowing verbatim from the Game Design deck: *"because as the designer, no one knows more about the project than you do."* True of the board artist too.
- The honest job-market slide sits here deliberately. Deliver it plainly, without reassurance — that's the whole reason it works.

**Have open:** the pitch schedule, posted in advance. Wall space or a projector for boards.

## Standing notes

**The three fairness mechanisms are one mechanism.** The silhouette test (S1, all term), EX5's naming test, and EX6's naming test all ask *does this read* rather than *is this well made*, and all three can be run by the student before submitting. When you name one, it's worth naming the others — the pattern is the lesson, not the individual test.

**The floor plan is the course's recurring tool.** S4, S6, S9, S11, S12, S15. Every appearance is a chance to make the systems argument concrete.

**Medium-open assignments are open by design, not by concession.** EX6 and EX4 assess identifying and controlling something; drawing is a different skill. Offering the options unprompted is the difference between a designed assignment and an accommodation, and students can tell which one they're being handed.

**Exit tickets are graded on existing, not on quality.** Ten to ninety seconds of work, one point, no make-ups. They exist to make the lecture land on a decision, and to give work sessions an attendance floor.

**Nothing in this file should migrate into a deck.** If a note here needs to fire at a specific slide, it may go in that slide's speaker notes prefixed exactly `Note for instructor:` — that's the only sanctioned exception (`CLAUDE.md` §2).
