# Module M3: Previs & Virtual Production
**16 slides · Recommended host: Session 15, replacing Deck 10 · Spec only, not built**

## Why this module exists

Of everything on the module list, this is the clearest **growth area** for the skill set this course builds. Previs departments hire people who can read a script, break it into shots, and defend the breakdown (which is the entire content of Sessions 3, 4 and 9) and they are considerably less crowded than feature animation.

It is also the honest bridge for the students in the room who came in through 3D and have been drawing on paper for four weeks wondering where their existing skills went. They went here.

The through-line: **previs is a storyboard that moves, in a space that exists.** Everything about shot grammar survives; what changes is that the camera becomes a real object with a position, a lens, and constraints.

---

### PART 1: WHAT PREVIS IS (1–5)

**1 · Title**: Previs & Virtual Production

**2 · The one-sentence version**
A rough 3D animatic, built in a real space, with a real camera, that the director can change.

**3 · Where it sits**
Script → boards → **previs** → shoot / animate → postvis → final. It doesn't replace boards; it's what boards become when the questions get expensive.

**4 · The four vis words**
**Previs**: planning the shot before it exists. **Techvis**: can we physically do it: crane reach, lens, set size, safety. **Postvis**: plate plus rough CG, so the edit can proceed before VFX finishes. **Pitchvis**: selling the sequence to people holding money.

**5 · Why it exists at all**
A camera move that's wrong in previs costs an afternoon. Wrong on the day it costs the day. This is the same argument as the milestone chain in this course, at three orders of magnitude more money.

### PART 2: WHAT CHANGES (6–10)

**6 · The camera becomes an object**
It has a position, a height, a lens, and a physical path. "A bit lower" stops being a drawing note and becomes a number.

**7 · The lens is now yours** ← callback to Deck 06
Focal length changes compression and apparent depth, not just how much fits in frame. A 24mm and an 85mm of the same subject at the same size are different *shots*. This is the perspective session arriving as a setting.

**8 · Blocking in a real space**
Your floor plan is now the actual set. Callback to Session 4: this is the tool's fourth appearance in the course and its most literal one.

**9 · Iteration is cheap in a way boards aren't**
Twenty camera positions in an afternoon. Which changes the *nature* of the decision: the constraint stops being how long it takes to draw and starts being knowing which twenty to try.

**10 · What doesn't change, and this is the point**
Shot size. Angle. The 180° line. Eyeline. Match on action. Coverage. Composition. **Every single thing from Sessions 3 through 9 transfers unmodified.** Say this plainly; the room expects the opposite.

### PART 3: VIRTUAL PRODUCTION (11–14)

**11 · The LED volume**
Real actors, real camera, a wall playing a rendered environment that reacts to camera position. The background is *in-camera*, not added later.

**12 · Why it changes the planning, not the grammar**
Content on the wall has to exist before the shoot day, which pulls art direction and layout months earlier. Boards and previs stop being pre-production and become production dependencies.

**13 · What it's good and bad at**
Good: interactive light, reflections, actors who can see the world, no green spill. Bad: extreme wides, fast whip pans, moiré, and anything needing more resolution than the wall has.

**14 · The roles**
Previs artist · layout artist · virtual art department · techvis · postvis supervisor. Several of these hire directly from the skills in this course, and the entry point is usually *breakdown ability*, not modelling ability.

### PART 4: THE BRIDGE (15–16)

**15 · What you already have**
If you can break a script into shots, mark a line on a floor plan, and say why a shot is the shot. You have the part that's hard to teach. The software is the part that's easy to teach.

**16 · Exercise: techvis one shot**
Take the most ambitious camera move in your final. Draw the floor plan, mark the camera path, and answer: *how high is the camera, what does it have to be mounted on, and what in this room would stop it?*
*That question is the whole discipline.*
