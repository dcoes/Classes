## What today is measuring

Ten minutes, and it is worth being precise because it is different from the midterm.

At class 8 the question was whether separate photographs could be made to agree. At
class 13 it was whether a surface still read once somebody else lit it. Today it is
whether a file can be used by somebody who is not in the room.

So the critique looks at the layer panel as much as the image, and the intent
paragraph gets read out loud before anybody sees the painting. A beautiful image in
an unusable file is a real outcome and it gets named as one.

The frame from class 8 still applies and gets one line each. The note is data. Do not
defend. Describe before judging. Everybody speaks.

---

## The critique

Ninety-five minutes, five minutes each, timed. Order randomized and posted before
class.

**Per student, in this order:**

| | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Instructor | Reads the intent paragraph aloud. Image not yet shown. |
| 0:20 | The room | From the intent alone: how would you have cut this file? |
| 1:00 | The artist | Shows the layer panel. Image still hidden. |
| 1:40 | The room | Does the stack match the intent? |
| 2:20 | The artist | Shows the image. Extension tier plays the move once. |
| 2:40 | The room | Two notes. One thing working, one thing not, both specific. |
| 4:00 | Instructor | The note behind the notes, and the one line about where this goes next. |
| 4:40 | The artist | One sentence: the note taken away. |

**Withholding the image until 2:20 is the whole design of this critique.** It puts the
attention on the decisions rather than on the rendering, and it means the weaker
painter with a well-reasoned file gets a good five minutes, which is exactly the
outcome the course has been arguing toward since class 1.

For the Extension tier, play the move once and only once. A second playthrough turns
the critique into a screening.

## The last thing

Twenty minutes at the end, and it is the closing of the course rather than of the
project.

Every student reads out the sentence from the class 17 exit ticket: something they
intend to own. They have had two classes to change it and most will have.

No discussion, no response, no instructor commentary on individual answers. Go around
the room, one sentence each, and let it land as a list rather than as a conversation.
Roughly eighteen sentences takes about six minutes and the effect of hearing them
consecutively is the point.

Then, briefly, close the loop on what the term actually was: three projects, each one
a job somebody gets paid for, and one recurring question underneath all of them about
who is doing the deciding. Two minutes. Do not summarize the whole course. Do not
give a speech about the future, because that was class 17 and repeating it weakens
it.

Last line of the term, and it is the same one the course has said every time
automation came up: the tool is a means of expression, and the person using it is not
an operator.

---

## Grading, said out loud

Three minutes, near the start.

Four rows on the P3 rubric. Thirty-five percent is the painting, meaning everything
the term taught. Thirty percent is whether the file is cut for the intent. Twenty
percent is the intent itself and whether the file serves it, and a vague intent scores
low regardless of how good the image is. Fifteen percent is provenance and process.

The Extension move is not separately weighted. A move that works raises the file
structure score because it is evidence the cuts were right. A move that does not work
costs nothing.

Say the tier rule one last time, in front of everyone, on the last day: reaching was
free all term and it was free today.

## Exit ticket

The peer notes log: every note given today, collected. Two notes minimum to two

## What breaks

**The timer slips.** Ninety-five minutes divided by eighteen students is a little over
five minutes each with no slack at all. If the room is larger, cut the instructor's
block at 4:00 rather than cutting anyone's time. Put the clock where everyone sees it.

**Somebody shows the image early.** The withholding is the design and it needs
enforcing on the first two students, after which it holds. Have the images loaded by
you rather than by them, which removes the temptation entirely.

**The last twenty minutes get eaten.** They will, if the critique runs long, and
losing them loses the end of the course. Protect the block by taking the time out of
your own commentary.

**A student did not deliver.** They present whatever exists and the critique runs
normally. Skipping the day teaches that not finishing is free, and there is one class
left to teach anything at all.

**The move gets played five times.** Once. It is a critique of a file.

**The room is exhausted.** Last day, final projects, four weeks of a block schedule
behind them. Keep the pace up, keep the timer visible, and do not add anything that
is not on this page.
