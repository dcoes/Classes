## What is different about today

Two minutes, said at the start.

At the midterm, the critique ended and the project was over. Today the critique ends
and there are thirty-five minutes left, and everyone spends them acting on one note
they just received. The point is to close the loop while the note is still warm,
which is a habit rather than an exercise: a note acted on immediately gets
understood, and a note written in a notebook usually does not.

The same frame from class 8 still applies and is worth restating in one line each.
The note is data. Do not defend. Describe before judging. Everybody speaks.

---

## Round one: the blind naming

Twenty-five minutes, and it is the best diagnostic in the course.

Every render goes up with no title, no author named, and no material stated. The room
writes down what it thinks the material is. Fast, about forty seconds each.

Then the authors say what they were making.

Where the room's guess and the author's intent agree, the surface is working, whatever
anyone thinks of the craft. Where they disagree, that is the entire critique for that
piece and it does not need any further discussion. A stranger looking at a surface and
saying "cast iron" when the author painted oak has produced a more useful note than
twenty minutes of technique talk.

Keep the tally visible. It is also the fairest assessment in the course, because it
has nothing to do with skill level and everything to do with whether the decision was
communicated.

## Round two: the critique

Sixty minutes, three minutes each, timed.

**Per student, in this order:**

| | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | The artist | One sentence: the material, and what the viewport told them. |
| 0:20 | The room | Roughness first. Where does this surface look like nothing has ever happened to it? |
| 1:00 | The room | Wear placement. Is the damage where use would have put it? |
| 1:40 | Instructor | The albedo check, and one thing to do in the next half hour. |
| 2:30 | The artist | Names the one note they are taking into the fix hour. |

The order of those questions is deliberate. Roughness first, because it carries the
believability and because students undervalue it. Albedo last, because the note is
almost always the same note and it is faster coming from you than from a discussion.

Have the maps available alongside the renders, so a claim can be checked rather than
argued. A surface that reads as plastic gets diagnosed by opening the roughness map
and looking at it, which takes four seconds and ends the conversation.

---

## The fix hour

Thirty-five minutes. Everybody at their machine, acting on the one note they named.

**The rules for the block:**

- One note. Not three. The student named it out loud and that is the one.
- A before render already exists. An after render is the deliverable.
- No new features, no new materials, no starting over. This is a correction pass.

Circulate. Some of these fixes will be four minutes of work and the student will be
surprised by how much it bought, which is the lesson worth having land: a large
fraction of what separates a working surface from a failing one is one map that never
got attention.

For the students whose note was the albedo, the fix is stripping baked lighting out,
which is unglamorous and visible immediately in the viewport.

---

## Grading, said out loud

Two minutes, near the start.

The rubric is five rows and it is in the brief. Thirty percent is the material read,
which round one just measured in front of everyone. Twenty percent is albedo
discipline. Twenty percent is the response to the viewport, meaning the before and
after and the notes file, and that row is the one most people will have skipped.

Baseline deliveries are graded on three rows, reweighted. A student who could not get
to Maya is not penalized for a row that requires it, and that is stated in front of
the room rather than negotiated privately afterward.

The Extension tier, two materials meeting, is again not graded against the Target
underneath it.

## Exit ticket

Before and after, posted. Two renders, plus one sentence naming the note you acted on.

The after render is from today, made in the room, in the last thirty-five minutes.

## What breaks

**Round one goes soft.** If the room knows whose render is up, the guesses get
polite. Keep the authors anonymous until after the guesses are written down, which
means collecting the renders in advance rather than having people plug in laptops.

**The fix hour becomes a work hour.** Students will use it to finish things that are
not the note. Enforce the one-note rule at the desks in the first five minutes, after
which it holds on its own.

**Somebody's note was too large to act on.** Usually "start over with a different
material." Convert it at the desk into something achievable in half an hour, because
the point of the block is completing a loop rather than completing a project.

**The albedo problem is still there.** For a few students it will be, despite class
11 and class 12. Their fix hour is stripping it, and they should watch the viewport
while doing it, because seeing the painted shadow leave is the moment the idea
finally becomes permanent.

**Nobody wrote the notes file.** It is twenty percent and it takes six sentences.
Give the last ten minutes of the fix hour to it for anyone who is missing it, rather
than letting it be lost points for a task that takes four minutes.
