## Where the tiers sit

| Tier | What you deliver |
|---|---|
| **Baseline** | One finished painting, delivered as a layered file that is organized well enough for somebody else to take apart. Either a new image or a substantial revision of P1. Photoshop only. |
| **Target** | The same, plus a stated shot intent (one paragraph: what the camera is doing and why) and a contact sheet of the build. The file has to be layered *for that intent*, which is a different thing from being layered tidily. |
| **Extension** | It moves. Six to ten seconds, After Effects, using the five operations from class 15 and nothing else. |

This is the only place in the course where After Effects touches a grade, and it
touches it on the top tier only. Nobody's pass depends on remembering it.

---

## The job

Every project so far has ended at a flat image. This one ends at a file.

The difference matters more than it sounds. A painting that gets used in production
is almost never used as a painting. It is opened by somebody else, pulled apart,
pushed through a camera move, relit, recolored, cropped to a different ratio, or
handed to a compositor at eleven at night. What determines whether that goes well is
not how good the painting is. It is whether the person who built it thought about
the person who would open it.

So the question this project asks is: **can you build a file somebody downstream can
actually use?**

---

## Layering for an intent, not for tidiness

A tidy file has named groups. A useful file is cut along the lines the shot needs.
Those are different, and the difference is worth saying out loud.

If the camera is going to push forward into the frame, the file has to be cut into
depth planes, and every cut leaves a hole behind it that somebody has to fill. That
hole is the work. A student who separates a foreground tree and does not paint what
was behind the tree has made a file that falls apart the instant the camera moves
half an inch.

If the shot instead needs the light to change, the file is cut differently: by
material and by surface rather than by distance, so a warm pass can sit on the stone
and not on the glass.

Decide the intent, then cut the file for it. Writing the intent down first is not
paperwork, it is the thing that tells you where to cut.

## The five operations

For the Extension tier, After Effects is taught as exactly five things and the
course stops there. This is a boundary rather than a shortcut: After Effects as a
craft belongs to Motion Graphics, and this project borrows the smallest possible
piece of it.

1. Import a layered `.psd` as a composition, retaining layer sizes.
2. Set the layers to 3D and push them apart in Z.
3. Add a camera and set two position keyframes.
4. Ease the keyframes.
5. Export.

That is the whole toolset. No effects, no expressions, no plugins, no motion design.
If you already know After Effects and want to do more, that is your business and it
will not be held against you, but the rubric is written against those five and the
grade is in the painting.

## Rubric

Totals 100% of this assignment.

| | Weight | What earns it |
|---|--:|---|
| **The painting** | 35% | Everything the course has taught so far. Value structure, light logic, edge control, material read, knowing when to stop. |
| **File structure for the intent** | 30% | Cut where the shot needs it. Holes painted. Groups named in a way that tells somebody else what they are for. |
| **Stated intent, and whether the file serves it** | 20% | The paragraph is specific, and the file matches what it claims. A vague intent scores low even if the painting is strong. |
| **Provenance and process** | 15% | Sources honest and complete. Contact sheet shows real stages. |

The Extension move is not separately weighted. A move that works raises the file
structure score because it is evidence the cuts were correct. A move that does not
work costs nothing, because the Target underneath it is what is being assessed.

## What usually goes wrong

**Cutting the file after finishing the painting.** By then, every separated element
has a hole behind it that has to be invented under time pressure, and the holes look
invented. Cut early, paint the holes as you go.

**Separating too much.** Forty layers is not a better file than six. Each cut is a
hole somebody has to fill. Cut where the depth actually changes, not everywhere.

**Confusing the move for the work.** A mediocre painting with a slow push is still a
mediocre painting, and the push makes its problems more visible rather than less. A
camera move is an amplifier in both directions.

**Writing an intent that says nothing.** "The camera slowly moves in to create a
sense of atmosphere" describes almost every shot ever made. What is the shot for?
What does the audience learn by the end of it that they did not know at the start?

## Why this is the final

Everything before this was a component. A composite, a surface, a map set. This one
asks for the thing underneath all of them, which is whether you can make decisions
that somebody else can work with after you have gone home.

That skill outlives every version number in this syllabus.
